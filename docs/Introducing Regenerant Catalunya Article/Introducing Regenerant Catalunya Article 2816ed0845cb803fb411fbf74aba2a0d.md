## **1. A Participatory Funding Round for Regeneration in Catalonia -**

A participatory funding program connecting local regenerative projects with global web3 infrastructure, by [ReFi Barcelona](https://refibcn.cat/)

[**Regenerant Catalunya**](https://regenerant.refibcn.cat/) is a participatory funding round dedicated to channeling resources into projects that are regenerating life in the Catalan bioregion. From conserving and restoring natural ecosystems like the Fluvià River basin to developing community infrastructure in L'Hospitalet de Llobregat — Catalonia's second most populous municipality and one of Europe's most densely populated cities — this initiative supports **on-the-ground regenerative work** across the territory while intentionally positioning itself as a living case study in regenerative finance.

Launched on **the 19th of November 2025**, the program is led by **ReFi Barcelona** as a bridge between local regenerators and international support networks. We provide a curated cohort of **11 projects** with funding, capacity-building workshops, and hands-on mentorship to experiment with **Web3 technologies and financial tools** for blockchain-based impact tracking and transparent collective treasury management. All program’s activities, materials and funding flows are to be made publicly available in english, spanish and catalan at the website – https://regenerant.refibcn.cat.

Both the funding pool *and* the activities of Regenerant Catalunya emerged through a mediation of local and global stakeholders interests. Established Catalan regenerative networks like [**Miceli Social**](https://miceli.social/) and [**La Fundició**](https://lafundicio.net) have together committed **€11,000**, which has been matched with `**$16,000**` **by global sponsors through the [Localism Fund](https://www.localism.fund/)** – bringing the total pool to**`€28,000`**, to directly support regenerative projects on the ground.

## **2. Web3, Regenerative Finance (ReFi) and Bioregional Finance (BioFi)**

Around the world, new approaches to finance are emerging that bring together **transparency, decentralization, privacy-preservation and democratic decision-making** in service of solidarity and regeneration. **Web3** — the decentralized web — enables communities to fund and coordinate shared initiatives without intermediaries, aligning incentives around collective wellbeing.

**Regenerative Finance (ReFi)** reimagines economic systems so that they restore ecosystems, strengthen communities, and redistribute value fairly. By experimenting with emerging affordances such as blockchain, digital measurement tools (dMRV) and local coordination mechanisms, ReFi wants to channels resources toward *regenerative action rather than extraction*.

Building on this, [**Bioregional Finance](https://www.biofi.earth/) (BioFi)** explores how territories (river basins, foodsheds, ecosystems, the so called “bioregions”) can develop their own **Bioregional Financing Facilities**: community-governed funds, local tokens, digital impact tracking and multi-stakeholder agreements that help value circulate locally and sustain long-term regeneration.

Together, Web3, ReFi and BioFi form an emerging paradigm for **supporting the Commons** — connecting global tools with local realities to strengthen the regenerative capacities of territories like Catalonia.

### **Regen Coordination: A Global Movement Bridging Scales**

![Regen Coordination Flows Ethereum (1).png](Introducing%20Regenerant%20Catalunya%20Article/Regen_Coordination_Flows_Ethereum_(1).png)

From the Web3 ecosystem, [**Regen Coordination**](https://www.regencoordination.xyz/) emerged as a global alliance dedicated to weaving together local experiments into a coherent movement. Founded by **ReFi DAO** and **Greenpill Network** with support from Gitcoin and Celo Public Goods, Regen Coordination spans a constellation of communities — including ReFi DAO Local Nodes, Greenpill Chapters, **Bloom Network**, **The BioFi Project**, **Ma Earth**, **AgroforestDAO**, and many others — united by a mission to drive ecological, social, and economic regeneration through open collaboration.

- **Open this toggle to explore key past programs**
    - **Regen Coordination Global Round (GG23)** — deployed a $96,000 matching pool to support 50 projects at the intersection of ReFi, Ethereum, and local social-ecological impact
    - **ReFi Mediterranean GG23** — the first bioregional quadratic funding round across the Mediterranean
    - **Regen Rio de Janeiro GG23** — bringing Web3 funding tools to Brazilian contexts
    - **Zazelenimo (Split, Croatia)** — pilot blend blending Ethereum-based participatory funding with a *3:1 matching contribution from the local municipal government*, enabling citizen-led tree planting through a public–private–community capital stack.
    
    In its first year, Regen Coordination raised and distributed over **$370,000** to regenerative communities globally. These experiments align with the growing **Ethereum Localism** movement, which embeds Web3 tools into place-based communities to coordinate resources, measure impact, and strengthen local economies — building toward a **"cosmo-local"** network where global resources and technology meet local knowledge and action.
    

### **The Localism Fund: Supporting Place-Based Ethereum Adoption**

Building on this momentum, and stewarded by **R[egen Coordination](https://www.regencoordination.xyz/)** and [**OpenCivics](https://opencivics.co/),** the [**Localism Fund**](https://www.localism.fund/) launched in 2025 as a dedicated grants program supporting **local networks and place-based groups** building practical models of political, economic, cultural, and ecological localism using Ethereum infrastructure.

**In its Round 01,** each selected local hub runs its **own local funding round**, directing matching funds toward projects that strengthen local conditions. Programs received **up to 2× matching** (max $20,000).

### **Regenerant Catalunya in Context**

**Regenerant Catalunya** is part of the first cohort of programs funded by Localism Fund  — receiving [the highest score among 30 applicants](https://www.localism.fund/round-01-results?v=2af06d2570f2808a8115000c66b6e0ab) — and aims to demonstrate the potential of Web3 tools to serve real community needs while remaining deeply rooted locally and co-funded by our partners Miceli Social and La Fundició / Keras Buti.

Catalonia is an ideal proving ground: the region boasts a vibrant [**Social and Solidarity Economy](https://social.desa.un.org/issues/poverty-eradication/social-and-solidarity-economy) (SSE)** with a century-long history of cooperativism, numerous organizations working on sustainable development and commons-based initiatives, and strong alignment with ReFi values like bioregionalism, ecological restoration, degrowth, and community governance.

By embedding Web3 funding mechanisms into Catalonia's existing ecosystem, we aim to amplify what's already working on the ground while generating insights and open-source templates for other localities. The learnings will flow back to the the global network and strengthen the infrastructure for place-based coordination.

## **3.** Introducing the Regenerant Catalunya**: A Living Laboratory for Bioregional Finance**

Regenerant Catalunya is designed as more than a one-time funding round — it's a **capacity-building process** and **living laboratory** for testing how Web3 tools can strengthen place-based regenerative work.

![image.png](Introducing%20Regenerant%20Catalunya%20Article/image.png)

### **How It Works: Local + Global Funding**

Both the funding pool and the program design emerged through collaboration between local and global stakeholders:

**Local Co-Funding (€11,000):**

- [**Miceli Social**](https://miceli.social/) (€6,000) — a cooperative service hub based in Ripoll (Girona) focused on **rural resilience**, catalyzing regenerative development across rural *comarques* (counties) by supporting municipalities and community groups in ecological transition.
- [**La Fundició / Keras Buti**](https://lafundicio.net/) (€5,000) — a multi-stakeholder cooperative and official hub within the *Xarxa d'Ateneus Cooperatius*, based in L'Hospitalet de Llobregat (Barcelona metro). Since 2006, they've linked the **urban commons** to bioregional practice through culture, education, and feminist economics.

**Global Matching (€16,000):**
Secured through the [**Localism Fund**](https://www.localism.fund/) — a new grants program supporting credible local networks building practical models of political, economic, cultural, and ecological localism using Ethereum infrastructure.

**Total Program Budget: €28,000**

### **Two-Stage Funding Mechanism**

**80% of the total program budget (€21,000)** is dedicated directly to participating projects and their networks, while the remaining **20% (€5,000)** covers **ReFi Barcelona’s coordination and operational work** for the program (June 2025–April 2026).

Funds are distributed **across two phases**, designed to combine baseline support with collective experimentation and learning, and tied to participation, reporting, and shared governance processes:

**1. Phase 1 — Baseline Allocation to Projects (Nov 2025–Jan 2026)**

Phase 1 focused on establishing a common baseline across the cohort. Each of the **11 selected projects was allocated €1,000**, for a total of **€11,000**, intended to support ongoing work while onboarding projects into shared tools and practices.

During this phase, all projects were onboarded to **Karma** for activity and impact reporting  and participated in capacity-building workshops. While these steps have been completed, the **actual transfer of Phase 1 funds has experienced operational delays** and is going to be finalized in the following weeks. Despite this, Phase 1 is considered complete from a programmatic and coordination perspective.

**Phase 2 — Collective Allocation & Network Governance (Jan–Mar 2026)**

As of early February, the program has entered **Phase 2**, with **€10,000 reserved for collective allocation**. Unlike Phase 1, these funds are **not pre-assigned per project**. Instead, they are intended to be distributed through **collective decision-making processes** involving projects, local partners, and other stakeholders.

During this phase, we are **in the process of defining**:

- How collective governance will be structured across project networks
- Which tools (e.g. multisig wallets, simple governance mechanisms) are appropriate and proportionate
- How funds might support shared infrastructure, joint initiatives, or differentiated project needs

These decisions are being shaped **iteratively and in dialogue with stakeholders**, through workshops, coordination sessions, and ongoing experimentation. Rather than imposing a fixed model, Phase 2 is designed as a **learning-by-doing process**, allowing governance structures and funding flows to emerge in response to real conditions and capacities on the ground.

This phased design allows Regenerant Catalunya to **test modest but meaningful forms of collective resource governance**, while remaining adaptive, transparent, and grounded in the realities of the participating projects and networks.

### **Tech Stack**

Rather than imposing complex blockchain systems, Regenerant Catalunya starts with a **minimal Web3 stack** and builds from there, prioritizing accessibility, proportionality, and readiness over technical sophistication.

**1. Project-Level Tools (Phase 1)**

All participating projects have been onboarded to a minimal set of tools for basic fund management and activity reporting:

- [**Karma**](https://www.karmahq.xyz/) – on-chain activity and impact reporting, functioning as a public project profile.
- **Web3 wallet on Celo** – secure self-custodial wallets for fund management. Given the context and user profiles, [**Valora**](https://valora.xyz/) was selected as the most suitable option, particularly due to its usability and social recovery features.

In parallel, a range of additional Web3 and ReFi tools were **initially explored and assessed** as potential optional experiments (including MRV platforms, impact credentials, and alternative funding primitives). While promising, these were **not formally proposed in this round**, either due to their **technical complexity**, **integration overhead**, or **state of readiness** relative to the current capacity, timelines, and risk tolerance of participating projects.

**2. Network-Level Tools (Phase 2)**

For Phase 2, the focus shifts to **collective governance of funds** at the network level. Rather than pre-selecting a full technical stack, the program is approaching this phase through **sensemaking and collective definition**, with tools to be chosen only after clarifying governance needs and decision-making processes together with participants.

Based on this approach, the tools currently under **active consideration** for Phase 2 are:

- [**Safe](https://safe.global/) (multisig)** – for shared custody and transparent collective treasury management.
**Safe** has become the standard for decentralized treasury management across the Web3 ecosystem. For networks ready to operate their own multisig, it offers maximum flexibility and security — funds can't move without multiple signers approving.
- [**Hum Community](https://hum.community/)** – as an emerging coordination and governance tool under exploration.
**Hum Community** meets a different need: lowering barriers to participation. For cooperatives like La Fundició, where member involvement matters more than technical sophistication, Hum's mobile app makes governance accessible to anyone with a smartphone. Members see euros, not tokens. They vote with simple taps, not wallet transactions.
- [**Regen Foundation](https://www.regen.foundation/) IDK** – the Institutional Development Toolkit is a cards tool and framework for sensemaking around governance and resources, conceived for regenerative communities.

Other governance and financial infrastructure tools — such as [**Gardens**](https://www.gardens.fund/) (conviction voting for community funding), [**Sarafu**](https://sarafu.network/) (local currency and commitment pooling systems), and [**Cycles**](https://cycles.money/) (mutual credit and circular economy infrastructure) — were **reviewed during the design phase**, but are **not being proposed at this stage** due to their higher coordination requirements, technical maturity, or implementation complexity in relation to the scope of this first pilot.

Overall, the goal in Phase 2 is **not to deploy technology for its own sake**, but to **co-define governance processes first**, and only then test whether lightweight digital tools can meaningfully support collective decision-making, reduce friction, and strengthen collaboration across the network.

## 4. Conclusion – Funding the Ecosocial Transition in Catalunya and Beyond

### **What we want to demonstrate**

Regenerant Catalunya is not a showcase of technology, but a **situated experiment** in how resources can be coordinated to support regenerative work in real territories. Rather than assuming that Web3 tools are inherently transformative, the program treats them as **hypotheses to be tested**—useful in some contexts, limited or unsuitable in others.

Through this process, we aim to explore and make visible that:

- **Local knowledge combined with external resources** can generate meaningful impact when mediated through transparent and accountable coordination mechanisms.
- **Impact and activity can be documented and shared** without flattening complexity or erasing local context.
- **Collective sensemaking and governance are often more decisive than the specific tools used**, and require time, care, and iteration.
- **Phased funding structures create space for learning and adaptation**, allowing both projects and funders to adjust course based on real conditions.

### **Looking Ahead: From Pilot to Infrastructure**

The learnings from this first round are intended to inform a longer-term process. If conditions allow, 2026 may include:

- **Regenerant Catalunya – Round 2**, with an expanded cohort and refined mechanisms based on the lessons of this pilot.
- **A replication toolkit** to support other territories interested in experimenting with participatory, place-based funding models.
- **Exploration of institutional co-funding**, including municipal and EU-level pathways, building on precedents such as [Zazelenimo](https://daotimes.com/this-city-pilots-web3-quadratic-funding-for-public-infrastructure/).
- **Initiating the work toward a permanent bioregional financing facility in Catalunya**, in conversation with **Miceli Social**, who already was considering it before this program.
