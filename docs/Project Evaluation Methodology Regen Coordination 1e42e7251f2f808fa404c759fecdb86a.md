# Project Evaluation Methodology | Regen Coordination

> **AIM**: *Transparent, rigorous, and regenerative evaluation standards to guide funding decisions and ecosystem growth.*
> 

## 1. Introduction

Regen Coordination believes that **funding decisions should be based on clear, evidence-driven evaluations** that reward real impact, not just good intentions. Our evaluation rubrics are designed to uphold **transparency, rigor, and fairness**, ensuring that regenerative projects are assessed consistently and meaningfully across the global ecosystem.

Through structured evaluation, we aim to:

- Support projects that **demonstrate tangible contributions** to regenerative finance (ReFi) and Web3 ecosystems.
- Strengthen **trust** among funders, communities, and participants.
- Align blockchain ecosystem growth with **measurable ecological, social, and economic impact**.

These rubrics will be updated periodically to reflect evolving best practices, ecosystem needs, and feedback from practitioners and evaluators.

## 2. Our Evaluation Principles

Our approach to evaluation is guided by five core principles:

1. **Evidence Over Intention -** We reward projects that show documented action and impact, not just alignment with regenerative values.
2. **Transparency and Traceability -** Evaluations are based on verifiable data and public documentation, making it easier for all stakeholders to understand why a project scored the way it did.
3. **Mission Alignment -** Projects must demonstrate how their work contributes to the broader goals of regenerative finance, decentralized technology adoption, and local impact.
4. **Equity and Inclusivity -** We recognize and uplift a diversity of regenerative efforts, valuing projects that empower local communities and strengthen resilience across diverse contexts.
5. **Adaptability and Learning -** Our rubrics and methodologies are living documents, evolving based on learning from each funding round, ecosystem feedback, and the growth of regenerative practices worldwide.

## 3. Evaluation Areas

We assess each project across **five key criteria** that reflect our [mission and north star outcomes](https://www.notion.so/Mission-North-Stars-Coordination-Council-1c32e7251f2f8070897fc38b8375509f?pvs=21):

1. **Increase Awareness, Engagement, Adoption, and Development of ReFi Web3**
2. **Increase Awareness, Adoption, Development, and Financial Activity on Celo, Ethereum, and the Ethereum-Aligned Ecosystem**
3. **Directly Create or Catalyse Local Ecological, Social, and Economic Impact**
4. **Resources, Maturity, and Past Funding Compared to Impact Delivered**
5. **Active Goals, Plans, Milestones and Objectives**

Learn more —> [**Mission, North Stars & Coordination Council**](https://www.notion.so/Mission-North-Stars-Coordination-Council-1c32e7251f2f8070897fc38b8375509f?pvs=21)

Each evaluation area is scored independently on a **1–10 scale** and contributes to the project’s overall score used in funding decisions.

## 4. **Detailed Rubric Criteria**

## **1. Increase Awareness, Engagement, Adoption, and Development of ReFi Web3 (Score: 1–10)**

**Description:** How well does the project contribute to increasing **awareness**, **engagement**, **adoption**, and **development** of ReFi Web3? This includes how well the project raises visibility, engages users, promotes adoption, and develops the ecosystem.

> ReFi Web3 refers to the use of blockchain-based tools (e.g., DAOs, smart contracts, ecological credits, onchain funding, local stablecoins, dMRV, etc.) to drive regenerative impact. Projects should demonstrate how they are using or enabling others to use these tools in practice.
> 

> This score should reflect how well a project contributes to the visibility, use, and growth of the ReFi Web3 ecosystem — particularly after August 1, 2024. Emphasis is on the depth and quality of engagement, strength of output deliverables and metrics, and demonstrated contributions to onboarding, education, or infrastructure-building. Small, well-executed activities that lead to meaningful adoption or builder activation may score higher than larger campaigns with little follow-through.
> 

### **Key Evaluation Anchors:**

| **Dimension** | **Strong Indicators** |
| --- | --- |
| **Awareness** | Large reach campaigns, high-view educational content, visible media presence promoting ReFi Web3 |
| **Engagement** | # of meaningful events and participants, number of community actions or contributions |
| **Adoption** | Measurable and sustained onboarding of new users, integrations with ReFi apps or DAOs, real-world ReFi use |
| **Development** | Training/building up new developers or entrepreneurs, launch of tools, growth of new local projects through support or funding |

### **Bounding:**

| **Score** | **Definition** | **What to Look For** |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **1** | **No demonstrated engagement with ReFi Web3.** No meaningful activities, outputs, or tools related to the ecosystem. | - No events, tools, or content related to ReFi Web3
- No output deliverables or participant data
- ReFi or Web3 mentioned only in passing, or not at all |
| **2–3** | **Some contribution to ReFi Web3.** Project shows some alignment but lacks clarity or effectiveness in actual engagement. | - Generic ReFi values referenced, but unclear connection to ReFi tech/tools
- Weak or non-existent deliverables
- Little real-world engagement or outputs
- Little indication of builder/community activation
- May be some introduction of ReFi concepts to local audiences
- Potential for ecosystem impact, but not yet proven or evidenced |
| **4–6** | **Some effective engagement.** Tangible outputs with moderate reach. Project begins integrating ReFi tools and builds community awareness. | - At least one high-quality event, guide, or workshop or Multiple events or campaigns with strong turnout (25–150 people)
- Output deliverables with specific participant numbers (e.g., 10–50 attendees)
- Some evidence of tool adoption, wallet onboarding, or protocol use
- Some local or regional traction, with measurable impact metrics
- Some evidence that the project’s initiatives have supported or inspired new builders or local projects |
| **7–8** | **Strong engagement and contribution.** Demonstrates multiple well-executed activities that drive real ReFi Web3 awareness, usage, and/or development. | - Many events, guides, workshops or campaigns with strong turnout (25–150 people)
- Solid evidence of tool adoption, wallet onboarding, or protocol use
- Strong deliverables (e.g. workshop recordings, resource guides, dashboards)
- Strong and consistent local or regional traction, with measurable impact metrics
- Strong evidence that initiatives support or inspire new builders or local projects |
| **9–10** | **Exceptional ecosystem catalyst.** Demonstrates leadership in onboarding new users, educating the public, and catalyzing new builders and ReFi projects. | - Projects that significantly **grow the ecosystem** (e.g., large scale ReFi builder programs, hackathons, incubators)
- Output metrics in the hundreds or more
- Clear causal link between project and new ReFi Web3 activity at scale
- Demonstrated influence beyond local level (e.g., public narratives, protocol development, international replication) |

> Scoring Guidance: Apply conservative scoring based on verifiable, recent, and meaningful contributions. Favor rigor and clarity over assumptions or good intentions. Do not reward vision or alignment alone — reward documented action and impact. Prioritize **substance over scale** — a smaller workshop with wallet creation and tool use may outweigh a broad campaign with no adoption metrics.
> 

## **2. Increase Awareness, Adoption, Development, and Financial Activity on Celo, Ethereum & the Wider Ethereum-Aligned Ecosystem (Score: 1–10)**

**Description**: How effectively does the project contribute to increasing awareness, adoption, development, and financial activity in Celo, Ethereum, and other Ethereum-aligned ecosystems? This involves fostering network health, user participation, financial activity, and ecosystem development. What evidence is provided?

> "Ethereum-aligned ecosystems" include Ethereum L1, Celo, and major L2s such as Optimism, Arbitrum, Base, and Polygon — along with projects that directly interoperate with or extend these networks in ways that strengthen ReFi outcomes. Contributions to other chains (e.g. Gnosis Chain, Cosmos zones) can be considered if they meaningfully support cross-chain ReFi infrastructure or interoperability with Ethereum-based tools.
> 

> This score measures how well a project contributes to the visibility, adoption, development, and real economic use of Ethereum-aligned networks — with **special attention to Celo and Ethereum L1**, in addition to other Ethereum L2s like Optimism, Arbitrum, Base, etc. Evaluation should focus primarily on **activity after August 1, 2024**, with older data weighted less.
> 

### Key Evaluation Anchors:

| **Dimension** | **Strong Signals** |
| --- | --- |
| **Awareness** | Evidence that the project’s activities have increased public understanding or visibility of Celo, Ethereum, or other Ethereum-aligned ecosystems. This may include targeted campaigns, workshops, educational materials, or media content that explicitly promote blockchain tools and concepts in a regenerative context — with clear indicators of participant reach or engagement. |
| **Adoption** | Evidence that the project has helped onboard new users or communities into Ethereum-aligned ecosystems. This includes wallet creation, use of Web3 dApps, participation in DAOs, or direct integration of onchain tools into local systems. Adoption should be tangible, with metrics or outputs showing real user interaction. |
| **Development** | Evidence that the project has contributed to the technical or infrastructural growth of the Ethereum-aligned ecosystem. This includes building or deploying dApps, smart contracts, or integrations; training new developers or entrepreneurs; hosting hackathons; or supporting others in launching onchain projects. Development of reusable infrastructure (e.g. open-source tools, smart contract templates, SDKs) adopted by others in the ecosystem |
| **Financial Activity** | Evidence that the project’s activities have catalyzed onchain financial flows — including increased transaction volume, funding round participation, onboarding of capital and users, and creation of local onchain economies. Demonstrates capital staying within Web3 rails (e.g., TVF), usage of local currencies, community treasuries, or ecosystem dashboards and metrics. Projects that enable local communities to retain value within Web3 are prioritized over those where value is immediately extracted from the ecosystem. |

- Publicly shares dashboards, governance data, or analytics to track ongoing financial and adoption metrics |

> Scoring Guidance: Apply conservative scoring based on verifiable, recent, and meaningful contributions. Favor rigor and clarity over assumptions or good intentions. Do not reward vision or alignment alone — reward documented action and impact.

**Description**: To what extent does the project directly **create** or **catalyse** ecological, social, and economic impact in local communities? This includes whether the project leads or supports sustainable impact actions and demonstrates alignment with local priorities and regenerative outcomes.

> This score evaluates the project’s real-world impact — including direct actions taken or catalytic support provided to others — that result in tangible ecological restoration, social equity, or economic resilience. Priority is placed on **recent efforts (after August 1, 2024)**, with older accomplishments supporting the context but weighted less.
> 

### 🔍 **Key Evaluation Anchors**

| **Dimension** | **Strong Signals** |
| --- | --- |
| **Direct Ecological Impact** | Evidence that the project has led on-the-ground activities that regenerate ecosystems — e.g., reforestation, biodiversity protection, soil restoration, carbon removal, or implementation of nature-based solutions. |
| **Direct Social Impact** | Evidence that the project has improved well-being, access, equity, or empowerment for local communities — especially marginalized, Indigenous, or historically underserved groups. May include local education, governance participation, inclusive decision-making, or reparative action. |
| **Direct Economic Impact** | Evidence of wealth creation, employment generation, economic resilience, or improved access to capital for local communities — particularly through regenerative sectors, solidarity economies, or circular/local value flows. Project supports systems change by building community capacity, institutional resilience, or long-term resource flows (e.g. cooperatives, mutual aid, land trusts, or decentralized governance). |
| **Catalytic Impact** | Project plays a strategic or support role — such as distributing funding, building capacity, or enabling other local actors to create impact (e.g., microgrants, training, partnerships, technical or network support). Demonstrates how project **multiplies** impact beyond its direct actions. |

### **Bounding:**

| **Score** | **Bounding Definition** | **How to Recognize This Tier (Linked to Key Anchors)** |

| **2–3** | **Minimal or implied contribution.** Project may align with impact goals but lacks specificity, scale, or evidence. | - May have completed / documented a single regenerative activity (e.g., pilot project, workshop, grant distribution, tree planting, beach cleanup)

- Ecological, social, or economic benefits visible and quantified
- Metrics and documentation are present |

- Supports other actors through microgrants, training, or platform access
- Provides solid documentation: photos, reports, outcomes, testimonials, or dashboards |
| **9–10** | **Transformational and systemic impact.** Project is a **driver or catalyst** for deep and/or distributed local change — across multiple impact areas or actors. | - Demonstrates integrated, multi-dimensional impact at both depth **and** scale across ecological, social, and economic domains with very well documented results and evidence of intersectional benefits
- Built long-term and operational new systems (e.g., community-owned assets, circular economies, shared governance)
- Catalyzes a network of regenerative actors with direct support (e.g., funding 10+ projects, training hundreds)

> Scoring Guidance: Apply conservative scoring based on verifiable, recent, and meaningful contributions. Favor rigor and clarity over assumptions or good intentions. Do not reward vision or alignment alone — reward documented action and impact.

## **4. Resources, Maturity, and Past Funding Compared to Impact Delivered (Score: 1–10)**

**Description**: How well does the project's impact and output compare to the resources available, organizational maturity, and past funding received? Projects that have achieved significant results with limited resources or early-stage maturity should score higher. This helps ensure that projects with fewer advantages but strong delivery are recognized and supported.

> This score is about effectiveness and return on resources — not just what a project did, but how that compares to their access to funding, team capacity, and operational maturity. Priority is given to recent impact vs. recent resources (after Aug 2024), but patterns of under-/over-performance can be considered holistically.
> 

> ⚠️ Note on Micro-Grants:
Projects should not be expected to provide distinct milestones and updates for every single small Gitcoin or similar micro-grant. Evaluations should consider whether the project provides consolidated reporting (milestones, updates and activity reports) within a timeframe that reasonably covers activity across multiple small grants (e.g., Gitcoin rounds), rather than duplicative milestone posts for each.
> 

### 🔍 **Key Evaluation Anchors**

| **Dimension** | **Strong Signals** |
| --- | --- |
| **Funding Efficiency** | Evidence that the project has delivered outsized impact relative to the amount of funding received. Demonstrates creative or lean use of resources. |
| **Stage & Maturity Alignment** | Impact is impressive when considered in light of the project’s size, age, or operational maturity. For example, new or emerging projects producing results that match or exceed more established orgs. |
| **Team & Capacity** | Smaller teams or collectives generating broad outputs or community activation — suggesting strong internal coordination and motivation. Recognition for volunteer-driven efforts or community-led initiatives that deliver strong results without formal resourcing. |
| **High-Resource Accountability** | For well-funded or mature projects: evidence that they’re converting resources into impact efficiently and transparently. Transparent documentation of how funds were used (e.g., public budget breakdowns, grant reports, dashboards, or fund tracking tools). Poor performance with high funding should be scored lower.  |

### **Bounding:**

| **Score** | **Bounding Definition** | **How to Recognize This Tier (Linked to Key Anchors)** |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **1** | **High resourcing with low return.** Project has received substantial past funding or support, but has not demonstrated clear impact. | - 3+ grant rounds with minimal activities or outputs delivered
- No clear link between funding received and tangible outcomes
- Limited or poor documentation of use of funds
- Vague or repeated promises without clear follow-through |
| **2–3** | **Under-delivered relative to resourcing or maturity.** Some impact exists, but feels underwhelming considering support received. | - Past funding or established team, but only moderate results
- Outputs don’t match scale or frequency of funding
- Reporting is inconsistent or lacks substance
- Missed milestones or low-effort public updates |
| **4–6** | **Performance matches expectations.** Impact is roughly in line with maturity level and resourcing. | - Average output and delivery given team size and past funding
- Some milestones achieved and evidence of impact
- Reporting exists, though may lack detail or consistency
- No major red flags, but no clear overperformance |
| **7–8** | **Efficient and effective.** Project is clearly doing a lot with limited resources or early-stage maturity. | - Small team or early-stage org delivering high-quality outcomes
- Single or small grants converted into measurable results
- Demonstrates good judgment and adaptability with available resources
- Tracks how limited funding was deployed effectively |
| **9–10** | **Outstanding resource efficiency and delivery.** Project demonstrates a “multiplier effect” — consistently creating high-impact outcomes from modest or minimal support. | - First-time or under-resourced team delivering ecosystem-wide results
- High outputs across multiple impact areas with low resourcing
- Transparent, well-structured reporting and accountability
- Could include community-led efforts with no institutional support but strong local delivery |

## 5. Scoring Methodology

Each project is scored independently across the five evaluation areas using a **1–10 scale**.

Our scoring system is intentionally **strict and evidence-based** to ensure credibility, fairness, and external defensibility. Here’s how it works:

- **1–3** → Limited or no meaningful contribution
- **4–6** → Moderate contribution; aligned but partial execution
- **7–8** → Strong, measurable contribution; above average impact
- **9–10** → Exceptional leadership; ecosystem-wide or transformational impact

**Important calibration notes:**

- **Recent Work Weighted More Heavily**:
    - ~70–80% of scoring is based on work **after August 1, 2024**, which marked the launch of the **Regen Coordination Genesis Funding Round**.
    - This ensures that scoring reflects the impact and activities generated **after projects received initial support** — rewarding those that have delivered meaningful outcomes with the funding and opportunity provided.
    - ~20–30% is based on **historical contributions** (before August 1, 2024) to provide helpful context without overly weighting prior work.
- **Evidence Over Intention**:
    - Projects are rewarded for **verifiable actions, deliverables, and outputs** — not promises, proposals, or alignment alone.
- **Use the Full Scale**:
    - Scores of 8–10 should be **rare**, reserved only for exceptional, externally verifiable projects.
- **Conservative Scoring**:
    - Favor rigor, clarity, and substance.
    - If data is vague, outdated, or unsubstantiated, score conservatively to maintain high integrity.

All projects must be evaluated by at least **three independent reviewers**. AI-generated baseline evaluations are used for efficiency but must be **manually reviewed and adjusted** by human council members before scores are finalized.

## 6. Who Conducts the Evaluations?

### **AI-Augmented Baseline Analysis**

For the Regen Coordination Global GG23 round, we use a **hybrid evaluation process** where **multiple AI models** are tasked with running a standardized prompt sequence for every project. This provides a consistent, fast, and reproducible baseline across the entire pool.

For this round, we leveraged:

- **GPT-4o (OpenAI)**
- **Claude 3.7 (Anthropic)**
- **Gemini (Google)** (experimental basis)

Each evaluation is run in a **fresh, clean chat thread** to avoid context contamination between projects.

Running multiple models allows us to **compare outputs**, check for consistency across different AI providers, and mitigate potential model-specific biases. For example, see [**Evaluation Comparison: Claude 3.7 vs GPT-4o**](https://www.notion.so/Evaluation-Comparison-Claude-3-7-vs-GPT-4o-1dd2e7251f2f80da9106e4ca6dedee5d?pvs=21) 

### **Human Coordination Council Review**

Once the AI-generated **Baseline Reports and Scoring** are produced:

- [**Coordination Council Members**](https://www.notion.so/Regen-Coordination-Global-GG23-1972e7251f2f802b9c8ef0a29fa8e6e2?pvs=21) individually **review** each project’s evaluation.
- Humans make **final scoring adjustments** based on nuanced judgment, context, and Regen Coordination’s North Star Outcomes.
- A minimum of **three human evaluators** must review and sign off on each project.
- Human scores are **averaged** to determine the final impact rating.

This approach is inspired by the emerging paradigm described by Vitalik Buterin as **"[AI as the engine, humans as the steering wheel](https://vitalik.eth.limo/general/2025/02/28/aihumans.html)"**:

> "AI can tirelessly and scalably perform large amounts of structured evaluation work, but humans provide the critical strategic steering — ensuring judgment, alignment, and integrity in final decisions."
> 

Rather than enshrining a single AI system, we use AI as a **high-efficiency analysis engine**, while maintaining **human stewardship** to uphold credible neutrality, discernment, and ecosystem integrity.

This ensures that:

- High-quality, verifiable evidence is consistently assessed.
- Biases or errors from any single model or evaluator are mitigated.
- Final decisions **reflect both systematic evaluation and human-centered strategic judgment**.

## 7. How Scores Are Used

The final evaluation scores play a critical role in **funding allocation** and **ecosystem reporting**. At Regen Coordination, we seek to balance both **community crowdfunding signals** and **rigorous impact evaluation** — creating a regenerative funding model that prioritizes real-world outcomes.

### **Funding Model: Hybrid QF + Impact Allocation (for GG23)**

For the **Global GG23 round**, we are using a **Hybrid Model**:

- **Quadratic Funding (QF)**:
    
    Rewards projects based on broad community support — emphasizing the power of small contributions from many people.
    
- **Impact Evaluation Scores**:
    
    Reflect the project’s **demonstrated regenerative outcomes**, technical ecosystem contributions, and resource effectiveness based on our five-part evaluation rubric.
    

**➡️ Final Matching Allocations are calculated by averaging both QF results and Impact Scores.**

This approach honors both:

- **Community voice** (participation, alignment, grassroots support)
- **Measured impact** (evidence-based contributions to ReFi, Ethereum ecosystems, and local regeneration)

---

### **Future Direction: Evolving Toward Impact-Embedded QF**

While we are using the **Hybrid Model** for GG23, we anticipate that **future rounds** will progressively shift toward an **Impact-Embedded Quadratic Funding (ImpactQF)** model — where **impact evaluations directly modulate QF matches**.

This model is based on proposals and early research from leading ecosystem builders, notably **Sharfy** and **David Dao** of GainForest and DeepGov, as outlined in the Gitcoin forum proposal:

> [Baking Impact Into The Quadratic Funding Matching Formula (ImpactQF)](https://gov.gitcoin.co/t/citizen-grants-gcp-impact-passports-and-impact-quadratic-funding-impactqf/19712)
> 

This future approach will:

- **Give greater weight** to **measured impact outcomes** over raw crowdfunding volume.
- **Prioritize regenerative results** while still maintaining a meaningful **community funding signal**.
- **Encourage projects** to not only raise funds, but also deliver and document real, tangible regenerative work.

By progressively evolving toward **Impact-Embedded QF**, we aim to create a system that better channels capital toward **sustained, decentralized, and demonstrable regeneration** — while retaining the openness and accessibility of QF.

- Strengths, areas for improvement, and risk considerations
- Baseline rubric scoring prior to human review

This approach ensures **full transparency** — not only the final scores, but the **rationale behind scoring** is openly available to the ecosystem.

## 9. Frequently Asked Questions (FAQs)

### **Q1: Why does Regen Coordination use such a strict and evidence-based evaluation process?**

**A:**

We believe that regenerative finance must be built on **trust, transparency, and verifiable outcomes**. By applying rigorous standards, we ensure that limited funding is allocated to projects that deliver **tangible ecological, social, and economic impact** — not just promises, marketing, or good intentions. This increases ecosystem credibility and aligns capital with true regenerative outcomes.

### **Q2: What if a project is very mission-aligned, but doesn’t have a lot of deliverables yet?**

**A:**

Mission alignment is important — but **action matters more**.

Our scoring methodology rewards **demonstrated contributions**: activities, outputs, adoption, ecosystem growth, and local impact. Early-stage projects can still score well by clearly documenting real activities and results, even if small in scale.

### **Q3: How does the Hybrid Model balance Quadratic Funding (QF) and Impact Evaluation?**

**A:**

In the Hybrid Model (used for GG23), a project's final matching allocation is determined by averaging:

- **Crowdfunding Signal** (QF Match — based on number and size of contributions)
- **Impact Signal** (Rubric Score — based on verifiable outcomes and contributions)

This way, projects that mobilize community support **and** deliver measurable impact are rewarded — without letting popularity alone drive results.

### **Q4: What is ImpactQF, and why is Regen Coordination planning to adopt it?**

**A:**

ImpactQF is an emerging funding model where **impact scores are embedded directly into the quadratic funding formula**.

This means that projects with greater demonstrated outcomes receive higher matching multipliers, ensuring that regenerative results — not just marketing or outreach — drive funding success. Regen Coordination plans to progressively evolve toward ImpactQF in future rounds, inspired by proposals from the GainForest and DeepGov teams.

Learn more here: [Baking Impact Into the Quadratic Funding Matching Formula (ImpactQF)](https://gov.gitcoin.co/t/citizen-grants-gcp-impact-passports-and-impact-quadratic-funding-impactqf/19712)

### **Q5: What happens if a project receives a low evaluation score?**

**A:**

A low score does **not** mean a project is unworthy — but it signals that **more verifiable activities, deliverables, and outcomes** are needed to improve their funding competitiveness.

Projects are encouraged to:

- Strengthen their documentation.
- Focus on measurable local or ecosystem impact.
- Participate in future rounds with improved reporting and results.

### **Q6: Can projects appeal their evaluation scores?**

**A:**

At this stage, we do not have a formal appeal process for GG23 evaluations. However, feedback can be requested privately, and projects are encouraged to submit stronger documentation in future rounds. Over time, we may introduce **formalized feedback and improvement pathways** as part of our evaluation evolution.

### **Q7: How often are the Evaluation Rubrics updated?**

**A:**

Regen Coordination treats the rubrics as **living documents**.

They are reviewed and updated:

- **After each major funding round** based on lessons learned.
- **In collaboration with the community and evaluators**.
- **In alignment with broader ecosystem trends** — especially innovations in Impact Verification, Passport Stamping, and ImpactQF models.

Our goal is to **keep improving** — building more fair, actionable, and regenerative evaluation standards over time.
