## 4. Phase Two — From Projects to Collective Governance

**_Putting the Remaining Funds in Network Hands_**

Phase One gave direct support to individual projects. **Phase Two shifts the remaining ~€11,000 to network-level collective governance** — giving Miceli Social and La Fundició the tools and autonomy to decide how these resources flow within their ecosystems.

This isn't just a procedural handover. It's an experiment in **decentralized stewardship**: can local networks use Web3 tools to manage funds transparently, make collective decisions, and build replicable models for other communities?

### What Phase Two Looks Like

**The Core Question:**  
How should each network receive and govern their share of the remaining funds?

We're offering two practical pathways — each tailored to the network's culture and capacity:

| Pathway | Best For | How It Works |
|---------|----------|--------------|
| **Safe** (multisig) | Networks comfortable with Web3 tools | Funds live in a multi-signature wallet requiring multiple approvals for any transaction. Transparent, secure, and self-custodial. |
| **Hum Community** | Cooperatives prioritizing inclusive participation | A mobile-first governance platform where members propose, discuss, and vote on fund allocation — no crypto knowledge required. |

Both options give networks **direct control** over their funds. Both create **on-chain transparency**. And both generate **open documentation** so other communities can learn from what works (and what doesn't).

### Why These Two Tools?

**Safe** has become the standard for decentralized treasury management across the Web3 ecosystem. For networks ready to operate their own multisig, it offers maximum flexibility and security — funds can't move without multiple signers approving.

**Hum Community** meets a different need: lowering barriers to participation. For cooperatives like La Fundició, where member involvement matters more than technical sophistication, Hum's mobile app makes governance accessible to anyone with a smartphone. Members see euros, not tokens. They vote with simple taps, not wallet transactions.

### The Institutional Development Kit (Optional)

Beyond these two fund-management options, we're also offering an **optional governance mini-program** built around the Institutional Development Kit (IDK). Developed by The BioFi Project, the IDK helps communities map their existing governance structures, identify gaps, and design protocols that fit their specific context.

This isn't about adopting a specific tool — it's about **understanding your own governance** before deciding what tools (if any) to use. For networks interested in going deeper into collective decision-making design, the IDK offers three facilitated sessions of governance mapping and protocol exploration.

### What's Already Happening

The transition to Phase Two is already underway:

- **Project wallets are opening.** Four of five La Fundició projects have set up their Web3 wallets; the final project is completing setup this week.
- **Network leaders are engaged.** Both Miceli (Oriol) and La Fundició (Mariló) are participating in planning sessions to shape how Phase Two unfolds.
- **Workshops are being scheduled.** We'll run tailored sessions for each network to explore their chosen pathway — whether that's a Safe technical walkthrough or an in-person Hum Community onboarding.

The goal isn't to impose a solution. It's to **validate what actually works** for these specific networks, document the process openly, and create templates other communities can adapt.

### The Bigger Picture

Phase Two tests a hypothesis: that **local knowledge + global infrastructure = better outcomes**.

Catalan cooperatives have a century-long history of self-management. Web3 tools offer new primitives for transparency and coordination. By combining the two, we're exploring whether these networks can:

- Manage funds without traditional banking intermediaries
- Make collective decisions at the speed of software
- Create replicable playbooks for other bioregions

The ~€11,000 at stake is meaningful but not make-or-break. What matters more is the **learning** — and the **proof of concept** — that emerges from trying.
