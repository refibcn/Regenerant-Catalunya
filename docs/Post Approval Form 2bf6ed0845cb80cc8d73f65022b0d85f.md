### Risk Factors & Mitigation Plan

Below we summarize the main risks we have encountered or anticipate in the Regenerant Catalunya local round, and the strategies we are using to mitigate them.

### 1. Funding shortfalls or delays

**Risk**

The main risk we have actually experienced so far has been **delays in the arrival of global matching funds** from the Localism Fund side. As round operators, we brought the local funding on-chain several months ago and communicated this to our partners and projects. The fact that the global funds were then delayed multiple times created uncertainty and some friction in our communication with local partners, who understandably expected the round to move faster once their own commitment was secured.

**Mitigation**

To respond, we have:

- **Adjusted the timeline** by shifting the closing of Phase 1 from the end of December to January, giving more time for funds to arrive and for projects to complete activities without feeling rushed.
- Communicated **transparently and proactively** with local partners (Miceli Social, La Fundició / Keras Buti) about the reasons for the delay and how we are adapting the plan.
- Structured the program so that **workshops, capacity building and impact documentation can still go ahead** even if disbursement is slightly delayed, so the learning and network-building aspects of the program are not blocked.

### 2. Local political, social, or regulatory considerations

**Risk**

In the Catalan context there is a **significant cultural aversion and/or skepticism toward crypto**, especially among grassroots and cooperative movements. Many participants have **little or no prior experience with digital finance or blockchain**, and even basic concepts such as *stablecoins* are difficult to grasp without a broader understanding of how cryptocurrencies and blockchains work.

Because of this, projects often have to **rely on trusting us as intermediaries**, rather than fully understanding the rails themselves. Building this trust and understanding takes time and repetition, and could limit how deeply Web3 tools are adopted beyond the pilot.

**Mitigation**

Our strategy is to:

- **De-emphasize “crypto culture”** and focus on tangible benefits: transparency, traceability of funds, reduced intermediaries, and alignment with cooperative values.
- Use **plain language and analogies** in workshops, and repeat key concepts over time instead of assuming one-off explanations will be enough.
- Start from **simple, low-risk use cases** (e.g. receiving stable-value funds, basic wallet use) before exploring more complex tools.
- Maintain a **high-touch, relationship-based approach**, leveraging the trust that Miceli Social and La Fundició already have with the projects.

### 3. Tooling, technical, or Web3 onboarding issues

**Risk**

The current Web3 tooling landscape presents several challenges for non-technical, grassroots users:

- Some tools in our original design have proven **not ready or no longer usable** (e.g. Karma GAP → Hypercerts flow, Grants Stack being deprecated right when it would have been ideal to showcase impact funding).
- **Karma GAP** currently requires a wallet-based login (no email/phone option), which raises the onboarding barrier for projects unfamiliar with Web3. The tool is also more focused on “tech-native” users than on grassroots organizations.
- There is a **lack of Spanish-language interfaces and documentation** for some tools, which increases cognitive load and slows adoption.
- On the wallet side, it is hard to find tools that combine **true ease of use with robust social recovery**; we need both, or projects could either risk losing funds or struggle to use the wallet.

**Mitigation**

To address this, we are:

- Building a **detailed, bilingual knowledge base** on the Regenerant Catalunya website with step-by-step guides, screenshots and short videos.
- **Abstracting as much complexity as possible** initially: round operators (ReFi Barcelona) and, where appropriate, local partner leads handle the most technical operations, while projects only need to perform a small number of simple, well-guided actions.
- Carefully **selecting the most user-friendly tools** available (e.g. Valora as a mobile wallet) and limiting the number of different apps participants must learn.
- Introducing a **Web2–first workflow** where useful: for example, asking projects to draft their 2025 activity report and 2026 plans in a shared Google Doc first, and only then supporting them to upload and structure that information in Karma GAP.
- Staying flexible in our stack: if a given tool clearly blocks adoption, we are ready to **swap it for a simpler or more accessible alternative**, while preserving the core learning goals.

### 4. Community coordination or engagement hurdles

**Risk**

Most participating projects are **small, overstretched grassroots initiatives** with limited time, attention and digital capacity. Risk factors include:

- **Limited understanding or buy-in** from some local partners and project teams about why Web3 tools are relevant to their day-to-day work.
- **Time constraints**: people are spread across many responsibilities, and year-end (November–December) is a particularly intense period for closing activities and budgets.
- Difficulty ensuring **consistent participation in workshops** and follow-up tasks, despite our efforts to adjust schedules and formats.
- **Hybrid format challenges**: online sessions are hard for some projects (connectivity, digital fatigue), while in-person gatherings require travel across Catalonia and more time commitment.
- The ecosystem is **geographically dispersed**, which makes synchronous coordination more complex.

**Mitigation**

Our approach is to:

- Put more emphasis on **asynchronous coordination** (shared docs, recorded sessions, written guides) so people can engage when their schedule allows.
- Offer **maximum flexibility** on our side regarding timing, duration and format of sessions, iterating week by week based on observed engagement and feedback.
- Be prepared to invest **more hours than initially planned** as operators to follow up individually with projects that need extra support.
- Explicitly **ask projects about their preferred ways of engaging** (time of day, online vs offline, 1:1 vs group) and adapt accordingly.
- Keep expectations realistic: framing Phase 1 as a **learning journey with modest financial support**, rather than a heavy compliance exercise, helps reduce pressure while still encouraging commitment.
