# Regen Coordination Impact Reporting

*This guide is designed to help Regen Coordination projects understand best practice for systematically documenting activities for reporting purposes. By following this guide, projects can ensure their activities are well-organized, impactful, and aligned with shared goals.*

## Why?

![Screenshot 2025-03-25 at 9.29.07 am.png](Guidance%20Regen%20Coordination%20Impact%20Reporting/be27d840-4f15-49a4-b1a8-2ad3093486c2.png)

- Regen Coordination is requiring **Activity & Output reporting** because:
    
    ✅ **It ensures funding is allocated** based on **tangible contributions**, rather than speculative promises.
    
    ✅ It **creates a shared impact dataset** that can be analyzed across rounds and funding cycles.
    
    ✅ It **strengthens long-term learning** and refinement of **impact-driven funding models in Web3**.
    
    ✅ It provides **reliable data** for **funders, DAOs, and ecosystem partners**, reinforcing **trust and transparency**.
    

GG23 will serve as a critical **experiment** in scaling **structured impact reporting**, setting a foundation for **future regenerative funding mechanisms**.

## Background: Measuring & Reporting Impact

[Impact Measurement and Management (IMM)](https://thegoodeconomy.co.uk/navigating-impact-measurement-and-management-imm/) is increasingly recognized as a critical tool for organizations, funders, and investors who are committed to creating meaningful, sustainable, and transparent change. 

### The Impact Value Chain

The **Impact Value Chain** is a foundational framework for understanding how an organization’s efforts contribute to meaningful change. It provides a structured way to connect **resources, actions, and results**—helping organizations track and communicate their impact more effectively.

By understanding and using the Impact Value Chain, organizations can strengthen their ability to:

✅ **Track progress** – Clearly see how their work leads to change.

✅ **Align activities with goals** – Ensure day-to-day efforts contribute to intended outcomes.

✅ **Communicate impact effectively** – Provide funders, stakeholders, and partners with clear evidence of results.

![Screenshot 2025-03-10 at 6.25.26 pm.png](Guidance%20Regen%20Coordination%20Impact%20Reporting/Screenshot_2025-03-10_at_6.25.26_pm.png)

![Screenshot 2025-03-25 at 9.26.29 am.png](Guidance%20Regen%20Coordination%20Impact%20Reporting/Screenshot_2025-03-25_at_9.26.29_am.png)

The Impact Value Chain helps map the progression from effort to change, consisting of five core components:

1. **Inputs** – The resources invested (e.g., funding, personnel, technology, materials).
2. **Activities** – The key actions taken to create change (e.g., training sessions, policy advocacy, tech development).
3. **Outputs** – The direct, measurable results of activities (e.g., workshops conducted, reports published, partnerships formed).
4. **Outcomes** – The actual effects of those outputs on people, communities, or systems (e.g., increased knowledge, new policies adopted, biodiversity increase).
5. **Impact** – The **proportion of outcomes that can be attributed to the organization's activities**, considering external factors.

**What is Impact?**

*“the changes (outputs, outcomes) that occur across the organization (within and/or across its programs) and with its stakeholders (including users, clients, partners, etc.) over a period of time (short term, long term) as a result of the organization’s activities.” (Source: [Innoweave](https://innoweave.ca/streams/impact-measurement/))*

### **Activities: The Work That Was Done**

**Activities** are the core actions that an organization undertakes to move toward its goals. They answer the question: *"What did we do?"* Activities reflect the work done to generate outputs, from hosting workshops to software development.

**Examples of activities might include:**

![Screenshot 2025-03-20 at 2.58.22 pm.png](Guidance%20Regen%20Coordination%20Impact%20Reporting/3457bc87-7297-4a76-a5ff-d6329bd0275a.png)

**Key Features of Activities:**

- They are actionable and meaningful.
- Activities may be simple tasks or multi-step efforts.
- They are the work that is done to generate outputs.

### **Outputs: The R**esults and Deliverables

**Outputs** are the tangible results or deliverables produced by activities. They answer the question: *"What was produced or achieved?".*

Examples of outputs include:

- 100 community members attended the workshop
- A new policy brief developed
- A research study published
- A new product prototype created

**Key Features of Outputs:**

- They are measurable and concrete.
- Outputs represent the *direct results* of activities.
- Outputs are typically within the control of the organization and can be tracked in a straightforward way.

Outputs should be reported with quantifiable details to show the scale or effectiveness of the activity. For instance, don’t just say “workshop held”; instead, specify “30 participants attended the workshop” or “10 new partnerships formed.”

### **Outcomes: The Real-World Effects of Your Work**

While activities and outputs are crucial for tracking **what was done**, **outcomes** provide insight into **what difference was made**. Outcomes describe the **actual changes** that result from your work—whether in knowledge, behavior, social conditions, or environmental improvements.

**For example:**

- **Activity:** A local node hosts a climate action workshop.
- **Output:** 50 community members attended and received educational materials.
- **Outcome:** 35 participants implemented sustainable practices in their communities.

Tracking outcomes ensures that organizations **aren’t just measuring effort, but real-world impact**.

**Short-term vs. Long-term Outcomes**

- **Short-term outcomes** – Immediate effects, such as increased awareness or learning.
- **Long-term outcomes** – Broader systemic changes, such as policy shifts or sustained adoption of best practices.

By recognizing that **impact is the combination of outputs and outcomes**, organizations can create a **clear, actionable** approach to measuring and maximizing their influence in the world.

# Regen Coordination Approach

While impact measurement and reporting requires time, effort, and careful thought, we are starting with simple and scalable solutions that allow projects of any size to begin tracking their activities and outputs. 

### Best-Fit Solutions

Effective Impact Measurement and Reporting is crucial for understanding and demonstrating the value and outcomes of an organization’s work. However, it is important to recognize that measuring impact is a **resource-intensive** process. The complexity of impact measurement grows, especially when tracking **outcomes and long-term impact**, which often requires deeper analysis, time, and advanced methodologies.

Given the variety of projects within **Regen Coordination**, from small initiatives to larger ecosystems, it is essential to find **best-fit solutions** for impact measurement and reporting that align with the resources and needs of the diverse network. The **depth** of IMM should be appropriate to the level of resources available, ensuring that efforts are sustainable and feasible while still providing meaningful insights.

![Screenshot 2025-03-25 at 9.28.31 am.png](Guidance%20Regen%20Coordination%20Impact%20Reporting/Screenshot_2025-03-25_at_9.28.31_am.png)

### **Starting with Activities and Outputs**

For **Regen Coordination**, we are taking an incremental approach to impact measurement by initially focusing on **Activity and Output reporting**. This foundational approach ensures that projects of all sizes and stages can begin documenting their efforts. **Tracking activities** (what was done) and **outputs** (what was achieved) are relatively accessible and require fewer resources than more advanced methods of tracking outcomes and long-term impact.

This reporting approach makes it possible for all local nodes, regardless of their size or resource capacity, to start documenting their work in a systematic way. It also ensures that we can gather consistent, quantifiable data across different projects, which will serve as a solid foundation for building more detailed reports in the future.

### **Building Toward Outcomes**

As we establish consistency in reporting **activities** and **outputs**, we will gradually move toward more sophisticated methods for measuring **outcomes**. Measuring outcomes involves capturing the actual effects of activities and outputs—whether through behavioral changes, improved systems, or the adoption of new practices. This stage requires a more sophisticated approach to data collection and analysis, including stakeholder engagement, surveys, or longitudinal studies, all of which require more time and resources.

By starting with activities and outputs, we ensure that organizations can begin to report progress and learn from their work, without feeling overwhelmed by the complexities of measuring outcomes right away. Over time, we will support projects to enhance their capacity to track and measure **outcomes** and **impact**.

Starting with activity and output reporting, our goal is to assess how projects contribute to achieving **Regen Coordination’s** **north star outcomes**.

### **Which Activities Should be Reported?**

Not all activities need to be reported. Funders or impact evaluators want to see your most significant contributions and the tangible outputs from your work. Focus on the **high-level activities** that are most impactful and relevant to your project’s core objectives. 

High-level activities represent the significant projects or efforts that define your work and contribute to your mission. They may involve a collection of smaller sub-activities that lead to a defined set of outputs.

These might include:

- **Major initiatives or projects** that demonstrate progress toward your mission.
- **Key collaborations or partnerships** that contributed to reaching your goals.
- **Events or campaigns** that had a substantial effect.
- **Ongoing programs or developments** that are integral to your work.

### Examples

![Screenshot 2025-03-20 at 2.58.22 pm.png](Guidance%20Regen%20Coordination%20Impact%20Reporting/3457bc87-7297-4a76-a5ff-d6329bd0275a.png)

### Nesting Activities

You choose how to define the high-level activity and what sub-activities might be nested within that. For example, if you organized a major event, that would be a good high-level activity to report. Within this high-level activity, you may provide some detail on the multiple sub-activities (e.g., arranging logistics, preparing materials, and coordinating volunteers) that contribute to making the event happen. If you run smaller, more regular event series, such as weekly community Twitter spaces, you might prefer to group these into one high-level activity rather than reporting them each individually. This ensures your reporting remains manageable and focused, rather than getting bogged down in every single instance.

### Start with your most recent and most impressive activities

Your most recent activities can be the best place to start and then you can work backwards from there to continue adding past activities that might provide further historical context… 

### **What Work Are You Claiming Credit For?**

It is crucial to make a clear distinction between the work your project did and the contributions from external partners, collaborators, or other projects. This transparency is important when reporting activities, as it ensures that your project’s contribution is clearly communicated.

**Guidance for Claiming Credit:**

- Clearly identify which activities your project’s team directly contributed to  (e.g., your team led the content creation, and the partner handled logistics).
- If your project collaborated with others (e.g., co-hosted a workshop), ensure transparency about who did what. You can claim shared outputs, but the activities and contributions should be detailed.

**Do not claim credit for someone else’s work!**

### Clarifying Node Activities vs. Other Projects

A ReFi Local Node or Greenpill Local Chapter should be a distinct entity. As such, it is essential to clearly distinguish between the activities carried out by your **ReFi Node/Chapter** and other projects that your team or its founders may be involved in or are supporting. This ensures that the specific contributions of the node are accurately represented.

If your ReFi Node/Chapter is distributing funds or resources to support another local project, these activities must be clearly **mapped** and **communicated** in your **Karma GAP reporting**. Reach out to the Coordination Council for guidance on this if needed!

### **Aligning with Regen Coordination’s North Star Outcomes**

To ensure that your contributions are accurately recognized and evaluated, it is important to report your Activities & Outputs in a way that **demonstrates alignment** with **Regen Coordination’s North Star Outcomes**. This not only strengthens the visibility of your work but also increases the likelihood of receiving impactful support and funding.

When documenting your activities, consider:

✅ **How does this activity contribute to one or more of the [North Star Outcomes](https://www.notion.so/Regen-Coordination-s-North-Star-Outcomes-GG23-Evaluation-Methodology-2aebf304370a811d8954d5c4056f2198?pvs=21)?**

✅ **What tangible outputs were generated as a result?**

✅ **Can you provide measurable or descriptive details to illustrate the impact?**

For example, instead of simply stating, *“Hosted a ReFi Web3 workshop,”* a stronger report would be:

🚀 *“Hosted a ReFi Web3 workshop with 50 participants, introducing them to on-chain carbon credits and local stablecoin systems. 20 participants created Web3 wallets and explored real-world ReFi applications.”*

By making the connection to **Awareness, Engagement, Adoption, and Development**, your report demonstrates **clear alignment** with Regen Coordination’s goals.

### **Best Practices**

Here are some best practices for documenting activities and outputs:

- **Be consistent**: Use consistent formats, terminology, and definitions throughout your documentation.
- **Be precise**: Avoid vague language. Instead of saying ‘several workshops’, be specific (e.g., ‘4 workshops held’).
- **Use visuals**: Where possible, include graphs, charts, or images to visually represent outputs or impact.
- **Update regularly**: Make documentation a regular part of your project’s workflow. Don’t leave it all until the end of the reporting period.
- **Engage stakeholders**: If stakeholders or collaborators are involved in the activity, get their input on the documentation.

### **Guidance**

- Report **tangible outputs** directly produced from your activities (e.g. guides, tools, event materials, recordings).
- Include **proof or links** where possible (slides, Notion pages, recordings, GitHub links, etc.).
- Group related items together when appropriate (e.g. “Workshop materials” instead of listing every file).

### Output Metrics

![Screenshot 2025-03-25 at 9.49.33 am.png](Guidance%20Regen%20Coordination%20Impact%20Reporting/Screenshot_2025-03-25_at_9.49.33_am.png)

### **Guidance**

- Choose **metrics that matter** to your project and the activity you are reporting — those that best reflect engagement, reach, or progress.
- Examples include number of participants, views, users, collaborations, or actions taken.
- Define your metrics clearly so others can understand what’s being measured.
- Be **consistent over time** — if you’ve reported a metric in a past round, try to use the **same definition and format** when reporting again, unless you have a clear reason to change it.
- Use estimates when exact data isn’t available — but be transparent and reasonable.

These metrics provide **measurable insights** into how well deliverables performed, ensuring **transparent and data-driven reporting** across different projects and initiatives.

### **✅ Focus on What Matters Most**

You don’t need to report *everything* — just include the **most important deliverables and metrics** that effectively convey:

- The key **products** and **offerings** delivered
- The most important **results** or **progress** achieved
- The **scale** and **effectiveness** of what was done

Aim for clarity and impact. Choose Deliverables and Metrics that tell the story of your work in a way that’s easy to understand and meaningful to funders and partners.
