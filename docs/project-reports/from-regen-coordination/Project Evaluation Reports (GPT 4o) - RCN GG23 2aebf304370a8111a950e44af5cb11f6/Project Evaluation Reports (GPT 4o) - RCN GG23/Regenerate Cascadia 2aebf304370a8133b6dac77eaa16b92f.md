Each activity is reported at an appropriate scope, with effective descriptions, tangible outputs, and clear alignment to the **North Star Outcomes**.

Two main activities are reported:

- The **DS4RE Learning Journey & Cascadia Cohort**, which provides a detailed account of educational and community development activities across the region.
- The **Cascadia BioFi Conference Planning**, which outlines a pending but well-scoped event with concrete deliverables, milestones, and projected impact.

Both reports are:

- **Appropriately scoped** as high-level activities.
- Include **nested sub-components** (e.g., cohort coordination calls, wallet onboarding, tool design).
- Aligned explicitly with multiple North Star Outcomes: especially ReFi Web3 engagement and local ecological/social/economic impact.
- Written in an accessible and **narrative-rich format** that supports context comprehension while still enabling evaluability.

The documentation is largely strong — particularly the **integration of media, internal resources, websites, and registration data**. It’s easy for evaluators to understand **what was done**, **why it matters**, and **how it connects** to the larger mission of Regen Coordination.

## ✂️ 2. **Opportunities for Streamlining and Simplification**

No major issues here — the current reporting structure is well-considered and **does not appear fragmented**.

The team wisely grouped all elements of the DS4RE learning journey under one activity, despite involving numerous sub-groups, events, and coordination efforts. Similarly, the Cascadia BioFi Conference is reported as a single high-level activity, with planning and implementation steps bundled together — this is an **ideal structure** for managing complex work while retaining narrative clarity.

No recommendation to combine or simplify further is needed at this time.

## 📉 3. **Missing, Incomplete, or Questionable Data**

A few areas for improvement:

- **Wallet Creation Metric**: The source for 20 wallets created is a YouTube video, which may not serve as a strong verifier unless it explicitly walks through the wallet creation process. A **dashboard screenshot**, **CSV export**, or **wallet address list** (anonymized) would offer clearer evidence.
- **Community Enterprises Incubated = 1000**: This is listed as a metric from Bloom Network, but **not directly attributable** to Regenerate Cascadia. This needs stronger clarification of *causal contribution*, or at least be framed as “network-aligned reach.”
- **Event Attendance Proof**: The “average 250 participants” figure is provided across 6 events, but **attendance proof is limited to a single Lu.ma link** with 12 registrations. While the number is credible, the reporting would benefit from more **linked registrations, attendance reports, or check-in screenshots** for other events.
- **Pending Deliverables**: The livestream archive, post-event report, and funding round results are all critical for full scoring but remain **future-dated**. A follow-up report post-conference will be necessary to **validate delivery** on claims made.

---

## 🧭 4. **Alignment with Impact Reporting Best Practices**

✅ **Activities vs Outputs**: Clearly distinguished throughout. Each report explicitly outlines what was done (activity) and what was produced (deliverables, metrics).

✅ **Output Deliverables**: Listed clearly — e.g., curriculum, call recordings, event websites, strategy documents. Many link to proof or explain where documentation can be accessed.

✅ **Output Metrics**: Most metrics are well-defined and bounded in time. A few (e.g., projected event participation) are forward-looking and should be confirmed in post-event follow-up.

✅ **Attribution vs. Collaboration**: The team does an excellent job here. For example:

- They clearly state that the DS4RE learning journey is a collaboration, and that Regenerate Cascadia is responsible for stewarding the Cascadia-specific cohort.
- Event collaborators are acknowledged with partner lists and links.
- No sign of inflated credit-taking for ecosystem work.

This is **exemplary modeling of impact attribution** and one of the strongest aspects of the reporting.

## 📉 5. Areas Where the Project Lost Marks or Could Have Gained Further Points

- **Outcome Evidence Post-May 2025**: A significant portion of the scoring in Criterion 1, 2, and 3 will depend on actual delivery of:
    - Number of participants onboarded
    - Funds distributed and how recipients were selected
    - Ecological/social/economic outcomes catalyzed
    - Event media and impact documentation
- **Metrics Validation**: Numbers like "1000 community enterprises incubated" or "250 average attendees" are impactful, but **require more granular proof** or breakdowns to validate in a funding context.
- **Ecosystem Financial Activity**: To raise the Criterion 2 score, the team could publish a **TVF (Total Value Flowed) dashboard**, grant round transaction log, or showcase **Web3 financial tooling in active use** beyond planning documents.
- **Open Data**: Providing a public-facing Notion/Google Drive folder with all deliverables, reports, and metrics in one place could increase transparency and evaluability — and serve as a model for other projects.
