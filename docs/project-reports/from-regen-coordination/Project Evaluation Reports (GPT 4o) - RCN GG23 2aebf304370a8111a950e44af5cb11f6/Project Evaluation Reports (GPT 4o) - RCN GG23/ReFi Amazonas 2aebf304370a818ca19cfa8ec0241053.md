# 🔬 Further Evaluation & Feedback

## 🧩 1. **Overall Reporting Quality**

ReFi Amazonas has provided a **clear and accessible reporting structure** with two distinct activity reports that are well-scoped and relevant to their core mission: community education around Web3 and environmental restoration. Each report contains a clear **activity description**, basic **output deliverables**, and at least one **quantitative metric**, which aligns with Regen Coordination’s foundational impact reporting approach.

The activities demonstrate **strong alignment with Regen Coordination’s North Star Outcomes**, particularly:

- **Local ecological impact** (via river cleanup);
- **Awareness and early engagement with ReFi Web3** (via the immersive tech bazaar);
- **Community-level education** (focused on youth and schools).

The reports are **concise** and provide **verifiable evidence** (photos via Instagram), which is appreciated at this early stage of grant reporting. They strike a good balance between accessibility and detail, without unnecessary complexity.

That said, the **volume of reporting is limited** to only two activities across a 6–8 month window. More documentation, especially post-August 2024, would strengthen confidence in their consistency and momentum.

## ✂️ 2. **Opportunities for Streamlining and Simplification**

🔸 *No streamlining needed.*

With only two high-level activities submitted — each with a distinct purpose and context (environmental action vs. Web3 education) — the current level of granularity is appropriate. The activities are not over-fragmented or duplicative.

If the project grows its cadence of activities (e.g. multiple workshops or river cleanups), it may in the future want to **nest these within programmatic reports**, such as:

- “Quarterly River Restoration Campaigns”
- “School Outreach & ReFi Learning Series”

But at this stage, the current reporting structure is effective.

## 📉 3. **Missing, Incomplete, or Questionable Data**

Several small issues limit evaluability and point to areas for improvement:

- **Low detail in activity descriptions**: The reports provide only surface-level summaries. For example, the March 2025 "Technology Bazaar" could have included:
    - A brief agenda or outline of the session formats.
    - Mention of specific Web3 tools or protocols introduced (e.g., any demoed dApps, wallet onboarding, NFTs, etc.).
    - Learning objectives or feedback from participants.
- **Limited metrics**:
    - Both reports only share a **single key metric** (attendance or waste removed). There is **no engagement data**, feedback from participants, or follow-up outcomes.
    - The waste collected is listed as “10” without a clear **unit of measurement** (10 kg? 10 bags?). Clarifying this would strengthen evaluability.
- **Missing deliverables**:
    - Beyond photos, no **presentations, workshop materials, or post-event reflections** were shared. These are relatively low-effort deliverables that could help build credibility.
    - The team could also provide **links to any digital content created**, even if informal (e.g., community WhatsApp discussions, shared educational decks, or video snippets).
- **Dates are vague**: While the activity summaries include approximate dates, **standardized time stamping** (as per Regen Coordination guidance) would improve clarity.

## 🧭 4. **Alignment with Impact Reporting Best Practices**

The reports make a **solid effort to distinguish** between:

- **Activities**: Clearly named and contextualized (Technology Bazaar, River Cleanup)
- **Output Deliverables**: Social media proof (photos) is included, but more could be done
- **Output Metrics**: Attendance and waste volume are quantified, though limited in scope and clarity

Importantly, there is **no evidence of over-claiming**. ReFi Amazonas appropriately claims credit for its own actions and avoids inflating their contributions through vague partnership statements or secondhand work. This is a key strength.

The project **does not yet report on Outcome Metrics** (e.g. participant behavior change, long-term community adoption of tools), which is expected at this stage — but it could begin exploring **simple follow-up indicators** such as:

- % of participants who joined future events of students creating wallets or joining ReFi channels
- Testimonials from teachers or volunteers

These would help bridge the gap between output and outcome in future rounds.

## 📉 5. **Note Areas Where the Project Lost Marks or Could Have Gained Further Points**

To improve future evaluations and potentially raise scores, ReFi Amazonas could:

- **Expand reporting volume**: Two activities across eight months is low — adding even 1–2 more (e.g. a second school visit, a social campaign, or a follow-up cleanup) would boost credibility and traction.
- **Clarify Web3 adoption touchpoints**: If wallets were created or specific ReFi apps introduced (even informally), documenting this would strongly support scoring for Web3 engagement.
- **Increase deliverables**: Even simple PDFs of workshop slides, screenshots of educational materials, or WhatsApp message threads could strengthen perceived effort.
- **Quantify more**: Track and report additional metrics such as:
    - % of students reached cumulatively
    - Social media reach of campaigns
    - Participant feedback (even informal)
