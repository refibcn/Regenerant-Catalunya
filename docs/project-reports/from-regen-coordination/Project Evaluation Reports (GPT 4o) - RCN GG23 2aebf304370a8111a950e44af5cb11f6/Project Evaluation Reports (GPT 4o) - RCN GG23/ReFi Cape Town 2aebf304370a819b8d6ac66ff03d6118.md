Activities are clearly listed with corresponding dates, descriptions, deliverables, and linked proof where available. The structure separates event-based activities and hackathon reports cleanly, making it easy to evaluate discrete contributions.

Alignment with Regen Coordination’s North Star Outcomes is **moderately strong**:

- There is **moderate contribution** to **Ethereum-aligned adoption**, mainly through referencing and integrating blockchain concepts.

However, documentation would have been stronger if:

Overall, the project delivers **competent reporting that supports a fair evaluation** of their early-stage contributions.

## 📉 3. **Missing, Incomplete, or Questionable Data**

There are several notable data gaps:

- **Missing timestamps**: While activity windows are noted (e.g., June–July 2023), exact dates for individual events or sessions within these periods are not always listed.
- **Metrics could be clearer**: For example:
    - The metric "Average No. of people tuned n" appears unclear (“800” followed by “10” — likely a typo). It is difficult to interpret the meaning.
- **Lack of deeper metrics**:
    - No reporting on follow-up engagement after events (e.g., number of participants who continued into projects or created wallets).
    - No indicators of ongoing community participation, Web3 adoption rates, or impact follow-ups.
- **Vague deliverables for hackathon**:
    - Although some outputs were described (e.g., team projects), specific project summaries, demos, or prototypes were not linked or presented as outputs.
- **Proof links**: Some proof is bundled in social media links or shared drive folders, but more direct, organized documentation (e.g., reports, outcome summaries) would strengthen validation.

### Suggestions for stronger future data:

- Include **post-event surveys** measuring wallet creation, protocol exploration, or onchain engagement.
- Provide **short summaries** of hackathon winning projects or demos.
- Clarify all metrics with clear definitions (e.g., what constitutes a "participant", "view", or "engagement").

## 🧭 4. **Alignment with Impact Reporting Best Practices**

- **Activities** (what was done) are reported relatively well.
- **Outputs** are **partially well reported**:
    - Some deliverables (like workshops and hackathon events) are evidenced, but others (hackathon project outputs) are not systematically presented.
- **Output Metrics** are present but could be **more precise** (clearer participant numbers, actions taken, adoption indicators).
- **Attribution vs Collaboration**:
    - The team appropriately attributes work to themselves and their speakers. They avoid over-claiming external partners’ work, which aligns well with best practices.

Overall, ReFi Cape Town meets **basic reporting standards**, but **could elevate their reporting** by better distinguishing outputs (e.g., hackathon projects) and including finer-grained metrics post-activity.

## 🔻 5. Note areas where the project lost marks or could have gained further points

- **Limited outcome and adoption data**: If they had measured and reported clear post-event adoption behaviors (e.g., number of wallets created, ReFi tools explored post-hackathon), scores for ReFi Web3 adoption and Ethereum-aligned ecosystem impact would have been higher.
- **Lack of technical outputs**: Metrics or deliverables showing prototype deployments or transaction volumes would have strengthened their ecosystem impact score.
- **Governance and dMRV** milestones still in ideation**: While these are strong future goals, lack of demonstrable progress on these areas reduced scores slightly under "Goals and Milestones."

### Specific recommendations:

- Implement **simple follow-up surveys** for events asking about wallet adoption, protocol usage, and ongoing involvement.
- Document hackathon project outputs formally (even a simple Notion page or Google Doc summarizing each team's work).
- Begin producing **public dashboards** or **impact maps** as they build out AI oracles and dMRV tools.
