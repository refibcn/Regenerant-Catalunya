## 🔹 **Criterion 1: Increase Awareness, Engagement, Adoption, and Development of ReFi Web3**

### **Regen Atlas Score: 7/10**

### **Rationale**:

Regen Atlas demonstrates strong, consistent engagement with ReFi Web3 through the aggregation and standardization of green crypto-assets. Since August 1, 2024, the project has:

- Listed 127 new green assets, bringing the total to 251 from 20 issuers across 10 chains.
- Developed and published a green asset taxonomy and listing framework, enabling standardized discovery and quality assurance.

# 🔬 Further Evaluation & Feedback

## 🧩 1. **Overall Reporting Quality**

Regen Atlas has submitted a **well-structured and largely effective** activity and output report across two main updates (Token Engineering G23 Update and Regen Coordination G23 Round Update). Their reporting includes:

- **Clearly scoped high-level activities**: Activities such as taxonomy updates, the Orgs Panel launch, EcoBridge integration, and database expansion are substantial and meaningful.
- **Use of standard Regen Coordination impact framing**: Reports are framed to reflect activities (what was done), deliverables (what was produced), and outcome-relevant outputs (e.g., metrics like number of listed assets, chains supported, certifiers added).
- **Alignment with Regen Coordination’s North Star Outcomes** is evident:
    - **ReFi Web3 Adoption**: Demonstrated through platform tooling and integrations.
    - **Ethereum-Aligned Ecosystem Development**: Highlighted by on-chain integration with Base and Celo, and aggregation of green finance tools.
    - **Catalytic Local Impact**: Supported through the Orgs Panel, though less clearly in direct regeneration outcomes.

Documentation includes **proof links**, public-facing resources (GitHub, GitBook), and platform demos, all of which increase the transparency and verifiability of claims. The timeline of activities is clear, and there is a visible progression in the project’s work since August 2024.

## ✂️ 2. **Opportunities for Streamlining and Simplification**

No issues found here. The project has not over-reported nor fragmented submissions unnecessarily. Reports are organized into a few substantial, meaningful updates rather than multiple redundant entries. Activities are naturally grouped (e.g., all taxonomy-related work or ecosystem integrations are bundled appropriately), which helps with coherence and ease of evaluation.

## 📉 3. **Missing, Incomplete, or Questionable Data**

While the reporting is generally strong, there are **several areas where clarity or robustness could improve**:

- **Financial metrics and user engagement data** are missing:
    - No user analytics on platform usage (e.g., number of users accessing asset pages, duration of use, asset interaction).
    - No reporting of **wallet activity, retirement volume**, or **TVF-like metrics** despite facilitating EcoCredit retirement.
    - These are critical to substantiating claims around adoption and financial activity.
- **Outcome data** is inferred but not explicitly reported:
    - There are **strong outputs** (tools, listings, dashboards) but **no user-side impact metrics** (e.g., user conversions, builder uptake).
- Some **proof links (e.g., GitHub repo, social metrics)** are helpful but could be better explained or summarized inline (e.g., summarize what’s in the GitBook or GitHub in the report body).
- **Collaborations (e.g., with EcoBridge)** are well documented, but the **respective roles and contributions** could be more clearly articulated. It’s unclear who built what, or how resources were shared.

## 🧭 4. **Alignment with Impact Reporting Best Practices**

The project follows impact reporting structure well:

- ✅ **Activities** are clearly described (e.g., "developed Orgs Panel", "integrated EcoBridge").
- ✅ **Output Deliverables** are mostly listed and linked (e.g., updated taxonomy, Orgs panel demo).
- ✅ **Output Metrics** include quantitative data such as number of assets, certifiers, and issuers.
- ❌ **Outcome indicators** (e.g., increased asset purchases, onboarding actions taken, user behavior changes) are missing or implied without tracking.
- ✅ Regen Atlas generally **does not overclaim** credit for collaborations. Their EcoBridge partnership is described clearly as a co-integration, though more detail on shared responsibilities would help further.

## 🔻 5. Areas Where Regen Atlas Lost Marks or Could Improve Further

To increase their evaluation score and improve future reporting, the project should consider:

1. **Publishing onchain activity metrics**:
    - Volume of EcoCredit retirements
    - Wallets connected or created through the Atlas
    - User analytics (DAU/MAU, asset page views, dashboard usage)
2. **Clarifying attribution in partnerships**:
    - In the EcoBridge example, explicitly state who led development, who handled marketing, and who maintains the feature.
3. **Integrating feedback loops**:
    - Consider collecting post-feature feedback from users or ReFi orgs integrated through the Orgs Panel.
    - Include user testimonials or adoption signals from the ecosystem (e.g., Celo DAOs mentioning Atlas in their reporting).
4. **Exploring light-touch outcome indicators**:
    - Even without full attribution, sharing “signal” metrics (e.g., # of ReFi DAOs reached out to via the Orgs Panel, or users accessing multiple org pages) could be helpful.
