While the reports are generally strong, a few gaps and improvements are worth flagging:

- **Lack of timestamp granularity** on some metrics — e.g., the “70 daily users of NYOTA” claim lacks a tracking period or dashboard link. Including dated analytics (e.g., from a block explorer or usage tracker) would improve confidence.

    - “No. of saving groups planned” is useful, but it would be stronger to include actual onboarding numbers after pilot initiation.
- **Missing cumulative impact summaries**: While discrete metrics are shared, a summary dashboard or chart (e.g., “Total waste processed YTD”, “# people onboarded to Web3”) would significantly improve evaluability.

## ⚠️ Where Attribution Could Be Clearer

### 🔸 **WashikaDAO’s Broader Identity**

- The documentation often **blurs the boundary** between ReFi Tanzania and WashikaDAO. For example:
    - In some places, WashikaDAO is presented as a **product/project of ReFi Tanzania**.
    - In others, it reads like an **independent protocol or ecosystem actor** (e.g., “WashikaDAO is live on Celo” with no mention of ReFi Tanzania).
- It is unclear:
    - Whether WashikaDAO is **legally or operationally distinct** from ReFi Tanzania.
    - Whether WashikaDAO outputs (e.g., contracts, app) are wholly attributable to ReFi Tanzania’s team or part of a broader collaborative or spinout.

### 🔸 **Token Economy (NYOTA) and dApp Tools**

- The NYOTA token and associated activities are reported as part of ReFi Tanzania’s ecosystem, but:
    - No specific team ownership or operational details are provided.
    - It’s unclear if NYOTA was built and deployed **in-house** by ReFi Tanzania or in partnership with external developers or other DAOs.

## 🧭 Recommendations for Improved Attribution

1. **Clarify Entity Relationships**
    - Add a short section (in Notion, project page, or reports) that defines:
        - ReFi Tanzania’s role (e.g., “core local node incubating WashikaDAO”)
        - WashikaDAO’s status (e.g., “a product developed by ReFi Tanzania with the intention to become a community-governed protocol”)
2. **Explicit Attribution in Activity Reports**
    - When reporting activities under WashikaDAO, clarify:
        - Who owns the code / deploys contracts
        - Who led user onboarding, design, testing
        - Who the collaborators were, if any
3. **Separate Pages or Linked Repos**
    - If WashikaDAO is becoming independent, create a separate repository or documentation site that credits ReFi Tanzania as the incubator, while also acknowledging other contributors.

## ✍️ Bottom Line

Right now, **attribution between ReFi Tanzania and WashikaDAO is directionally correct but lacks explicit framing**. Most readers will reasonably infer they’re part of the same team, but it’s not formally clarified. If ReFi Tanzania wants to maintain clear credit — especially in grant evaluation and funding — they should take steps to document this relationship more precisely moving forward.
