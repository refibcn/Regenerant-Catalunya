# Eligibility and Criteria

## Eligibility

*(Sequential checks to proceed to next step)*

- 1 - Completed application sufficiently (yes / no)
- 2 - Meets Program Criteria (yes / no)
- 3 - Meets Round Criteria  (yes / no)
- 4 - Local Program Proceeding and Matching Funds Confirmed (yes / no)

**Result** = funds dispersed to grantees

Note – The first week of every month funds are dispersed, until funds are depleted.

## Program Criteria

- **Local Focus** - Grantee is anchored in a place-based context advancing political, economic, cultural, and ecological localism.
- **Ethereum Alignment** — Grantee demonstrates how their work contributes to the adoption, legitimacy, or utility of Ethereum, even if the benefit is second-order (e.g. leveraging Ethereum-based tooling, infrastructure, or coordination mechanisms).
- **Public Goods Orientation** - Grantee aims to utilize and advance open infrastructure and resources that offer public benefit, strengthening communities and economies.
- **Impact & On-chain Reporting** - Grantee demonstrates a plan to track and report both local outcomes (political, ecological, economic, cultural) and Ethereum on-chain activity (e.g. use of coordination tools, attestations, or hypercerts).
- **Readiness & Capability** - Grantee shows overall readiness to fulfill responsibilities as a grantee, with a credible team or coalition that has relevant experience, trusted community relationships, and the capacity to operate governance, partnerships, outreach, treasury, and reporting.
- **Ethical & Compliance Alignment** - Grantee commits to OpenCivics Ethical Standards, participate in collaborative accountability protocols, and passes required compliance checks (e.g. OFAC).

## Round Criteria

- Local Grants Program ready to start by January 2026 (yes / no)
- Minimum of $5k of local co-funding raised/committed (yes / no)
- Program operator compensation sufficiently accounted for (yes / no)
- Round evaluation rubric score of “qualified candidate” or above (yes / no)
- Minimum of 4 experts have completed evaluation (yes / no)

# Evaluation Rubric

### **1. Program Scope & Strategic Alignment (Weight: 30%)**

**Evaluates how well the grant program’s scope, design, and objectives align with the mission of Localism Fund and the broader Ethereum ecosystem.** This includes clarity of goals, local relevance, connection to broader systemic outcomes, and feasibility of implementation within the proposed timeline. Reviewers look for grounded, well-structured proposals that demonstrate credible local leadership and potential for wider learning or replication.

- **Excellent (4 points):**
    - Clear, well-structured articulation of funding targets, beneficiaries, and strategic rationale
    - Strong grounding in local context with compelling rationale for local leadership
    - Direct connection between local objectives and broader Ethereum adoption outcomes
    - Optimal resource-to-impact ratio with realistic and appropriately sized project portfolio
    - Feasible, time-bound implementation plan (Q4 2025 / Q1 2026)
    - Demonstrates potential for replication, scalability, or knowledge-sharing beyond the immediate community
- **Good (3 points):**
    - Well-defined scope with minor gaps in strategic articulation or local rationale
    - Generally appropriate resource allocation, with slight over/under-sizing of project portfolio
    - Clear timeline for late 2025 / early 2026 launch, with some areas needing refinement
    - Some indication of replicability or broader learning, though not fully developed
- **Satisfactory (2 points):**
    - Basic scope definition present but lacks depth, clarity, or strong local grounding
    - Resource allocation concerns or signs of misalignment between inputs and intended impact
    - Uncertain or incomplete timeline for launch and delivery
    - Limited connection to broader ecosystem goals or replicability potential
- **Needs Improvement (1 point):**
    - Vague or poorly defined program scope with little strategic rationale
    - Weak or missing justification for local leadership
    - Significant resource–impact misalignment or unrealistic portfolio sizing
    - Unclear or unrealistic timeline for launch readiness
    - No clear pathway to broader ecosystem relevance or learning

### **2. Local Co-Funding & Financial Sustainability (Weight: 30%)**

**Evaluates the financial readiness and sustainability of the proposed local grant program.** This includes the strength, credibility, and diversity of co-funding sources; the presence of a sound and transparent budget; and evidence that the program can operate sustainably through Operator Fees or other long-term mechanisms.

- **Excellent (4 points):**
    - Strong co-funding secured (e.g. $15k+ as a typical benchmark)
    - Funding comes from diverse and credible sources, with evidence of local legitimacy and community buy-in (e.g., municipalities, NGOs, civic institutions)
    - Comprehensive and transparent budget, including Fair Fees and sustainability planning
    - Evidence of a strong pipeline (e.g., signed commitments, MOUs, or advanced negotiations) for additional funding
- **Good (3 points):**
    - Moderate co-funding secured ($10–14k typical)
    - Some diversity of sources, with at least partial local or institutional buy-in
    - Reasonable operational planning, budget allocations and some sustainability considerations
- **Satisfactory (2 points):**
    - Minimum co-funding met ($5–10k typical)
    - Limited source diversity or weaker credibility of funders
    - Basic operational budget
- **Needs Improvement (1 point):**
    - Insufficient co-funding (<$5k typical)
    - Over-reliance on a single, less credible source
    - Weak or missing operational planning

### **3. Round Operations, Mechanisms & Impact Framework (Weight: 20%)**

**Evaluates the applicant’s ability to design, operate, and measure a coherent, technically sound local grant programusing credible mechanisms and transparent Web3 tools.** This includes operational readiness, clarity on how funds will be distributed, integration of Web3 infrastructure, and a thoughtful approach to capturing and communicating impact (onchain and offchain).

- **Excellent (4 points):**
    - Clearly articulated **round design and operational plan**, including how projects will apply, be evaluated, selected, and funded
    - Well-defined **funding mechanism** (e.g. direct grants, QF, conviction voting, milestone-based payouts) with thoughtful rationale for its use
    - **Realistic and detailed timeline** covering all key phases (fundraising, intake, evaluation, disbursement, and reporting)
    - Strong integration of **Web3-native tools and infrastructure** (e.g. Karma GAP, Gardens, Prosperity Pass, EAS attestations) for transparency and accountability
    - Comprehensive and innovative **impact measurement approach**, combining onchain and offchain metrics (qualitative, quantitative, and narrative)
    - Demonstrates openness to experimentation and a learning mindset that will generate replicable insights for other local programs
- **Good (3 points):**
    - Solid operational plan with minor gaps in timeline, mechanism clarity, or impact framework
    - Chosen funding mechanism is appropriate but not yet fully defined
    - Clear intention to use Web3 tools and reporting frameworks, though integration plans may still be maturing
    - Balanced approach to measuring outcomes and documenting learnings
    - Demonstrates some potential for broader applicability or knowledge sharing
- **Satisfactory (2 points):**
    - Basic understanding of how the local round will operate, but missing clarity on one or more stages (intake, evaluation, disbursement, etc.)
    - Limited definition of mechanism or rationale for chosen tools
    - General mention of impact goals without a concrete measurement or reporting plan
    - Minimal evidence of onchain integration or experimentation with Web3 tooling
    - Timeline lacks detail or coherence
- **Needs Improvement (1 point):**
    - Vague or incomplete operational plan with no clear mechanism or funding flow described
    - Unclear or unrealistic timeline for implementation and reporting
    - No mention of Web3 tool usage or integration
    - Weak or absent plan for measuring impact or documenting learnings
    - Little evidence of feasibility or readiness to begin operations by January 2026

### **4. Team Capacity & Community Anchoring (Weight: 20%)**

**Evaluates the strength, credibility, and readiness of the team or organization to deliver the proposed grant program.** Reviewers look for operational competence, established community relationships, effective governance, and demonstrated trust within their local ecosystem. The goal is to ensure programs are led by capable, locally rooted actors who can steward funds responsibly.

- **Excellent (4 points):**
    - Proven track record in funding program management across all operational areas
    - Strong anchoring in established hub/chapter with deep community trust
    - Demonstrated governance, partnership, and treasury management experience
    - Clear operational structure and role distribution
- **Good (3 points):**
    - Solid operational experience with some gaps
    - Good community connections and established relationships
    - Adequate governance and management capabilities
- **Satisfactory (2 points):**
    - Basic operational capacity with limited track record
    - Emerging community relationships
    - Fundamental management skills present
- **Needs Improvement (1 point):**
    - Insufficient operational experience
    - Weak community anchoring or trust relationships
    - Unclear governance and management structure

### **Scoring & Matching Framework**

- Up to **2x matching awarded**, max $20k per grantee
- Total Score: 13-16 points / ~**x2 matching** - Strong candidate for approval and higher allocation
- Total Score: 10-12 points / ~**x1.5 matching** - Qualified candidate with standard allocation consideration
- Total Score: 7-9 points ****- Conditional approval pending improvements
- Total Score: Below 7 - Requires significant development before reapplication

# Attested Expert Network (Experts)

- **Round Operators (operators):** Monty, Patricia, and Benjamin
- **Expert Evaluators (experts):** 9–12 members from the Attested Expert Network
- Experts bring proven experience in grant-making, Web3-based programs, Ethereum Localism, and/or community-led funding initiatives.
- Their role is to **evaluate applications, provide feedback, support grantees, and provide feedback on tooling and program design.**
- $5,500 + 10,000 CELO (~$3600) will be distributed to the expert network for their contributions during this first cycle.
- **Localism Fund Expert Badge** for the round**:** Each expert will receive an on-chain credential that reflects their contribution tier for the round. This badge, when utilized on Prosperity Pass, boosts their reputation in the wider ecosystem, unlocks eligibility for additional rewards, and creates a portable record of contribution that can be recognized across future rounds and networks. Beyond Prosperity Pass, the badge is an EAS based acknowledgement of contribution.

The expert network provides crucial contributions to round and broader program by:

- **Application Review & Evaluation:** Scoring applicants against the evaluation rubric and leaving qualitative feedback to signal priorities for funding allocation. This process surfaces collective intelligence and helps guide operator decisions.
- **Community Support:** Assisting grantees in successfully implementing their local programs by sharing guidance, playbooks, and best practices; providing hands-on help with Web3 mechanisms and tooling; and offering operational mentorship to strengthen local delivery capacity.
- **Tooling Feedback & Improvement:** Collecting feedback and generating concrete ideas for improving the usability, accessibility, and effectiveness of the Web3 tools local communities rely on to run their programs.
