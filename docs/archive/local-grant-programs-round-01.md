# Local Grant Programs | Round 01

Welcome to the public coordination portal for the Local Grant Programs Round (01)

## Overview

**Local Grant Programs** is the first funding round of the **Localism Fund**, a new grants program supporting **credible local networks and place-based groups** who are building practical models of political, economic, cultural, and ecological localism.

Through this round, we’re funding **locally-led grant programs** — not just single projects — that bring Ethereum tools and values into the heart of communities to coordinate resources, governance, and regeneration on the ground.

## Key Info

- **Total Matching Pool:** $125,000
- **Grant Range:** $5,000 – $20,000 per local program (matching basis)
- **Applications Now Open!** See **[ **How to Apply**](https://www.notion.so/How-to-Apply-28406d2570f28067a25afe02620b80c5?pvs=21)**
- **First Grantees Selected**: 1st week of November
- **Round Operators:** Patricia Parkinson & Benjamin Life (OpenCivics), Monty Merlin (Regen Coordination)
- **Local Program Delivery Period:** Q4 2025 – Q1 2026

## What We’re Funding

We’re funding **local grant programs** — community-led initiatives that bring people together to fund what matters most in their own local area.

Each selected hub will run its **own local funding round**, deciding how to direct matching funds toward projects that strengthen their local political, economic, cultural, and ecological conditions.

The goal is to **demonstrate how Ethereum tools can help communities coordinate and share resources** more transparently, fairly, and effectively.

### **In Practice**

Depending on the local context, your program might focus on supporting:

- 🪴 **Regenerative initiatives, NGOs & non-profits -** Ecological restoration, local farming, recycling, or social inclusion programs.
- 🏛️ **Civic initiatives -** Municipal pilots, community currencies, participatory budgeting, or local digital governance.
- 🎓 **Education & Learning Spaces -** Schools, labs, and workshops teaching regenerative economics or Ethereum-based coordination.
- 💻 **Builder & Developer Communities -** Hackathons, open-source collectives, and developer groups creating public tools for local use.
- 🎨 **Arts, Media & Culture -** Storytelling, local journalism, cultural regeneration, and creative collaborations.
- 🌾 **Entrepreneurship & Local Businesses -** Co-ops, small business incubators, and circular economy projects creating local livelihoods.
- 🔗 **Web3-Aligned Local Networks -** ReFi nodes, Greenpill chapters, or other DAOs using Ethereum to fund local impact.

Each local program will use a mix of **Web3 tools** (e.g. wallets, attestations, stablecoins, impact tracking, funding and governance tools) to manage payouts as well as coordinate decision-making and funding in the open. **Don't worry if this is new to you**, round operators are here to support!

Beyond the tech, what really matters is that these programs **reflect local priorities** and **empower people closest to the challenges** to decide where resources should go.

## Eligibility Snapshot

## Round Lifecycle

The Local Grant Programs Round follows a transparent, step-by-step process designed to make participation clear, fair, and rewarding for both grantees and reviewers. Each phase builds toward a simple goal: empowering credible local networks to run effective, Ethereum-aligned grant programs that strengthen their communities.

### Stage 1 – Announcement & Intake

The round officially launches in early October 2025 in alignment with **Gitcoin’s GG24** cycle.

Applications opened **October 14th** via the [Karma](https://app.karmahq.xyz/localism-fund/programs/975), which serves as the submission and evaluation system.

Applicants submit their **program application**, including their intent, co-funding details, team capacity, and budget.

**Round Operators** review all submissions for completeness and eligibility, ensuring that each meets the baseline **Program and Round Criteria** before advancing.

### Stage 2 – Evaluation & Selection

Eligible applications are reviewed by an **Attested Expert Network**, a group of 9–12 peer-vouched evaluators recognized through on-chain credentials.

Each expert independently scores applications using the **Evaluation Rubric**, which measures:

1. **Program Scope & Strategic Alignment**
2. **Local Co-Funding & Financial Sustainability**
3. **Impact Measurement & Web3 Integration**
4. **Team Capacity & Community Anchoring**

Experts also leave **qualitative feedback** to guide applicants and inform operator decisions.

At least **four expert reviews** are required per program before selection.

Once a program reaches the **“qualified” threshold**, it is marked as _conditionally committed_ — pending final confirmation of co-funding and readiness to launch. Operators then verify these details before confirming final grantees.

### Stage 3 – Disbursement & Execution

Funding is released on a **rolling basis**, beginning in November 2025.

Disbursements occur in the **first week of each month** and continue until the pool is fully allocated.

Each grantee receives their matching funds (up to **2× co-funding**, capped at **$20,000**) directly into their program wallet.

Operators will maintain **open reporting** of all disbursements, providing transparency into fund flows and commitments.

### Stage 4 – Program Delivery & Impact

Once funded, each Local Program begins its own **local grantmaking process** — allocating funds to community initiatives aligned with their mission and local priorities.

During this phase:

- Grantees document outcomes, learning, and progress through **on-chain attestations** and qualitative reports.
- The **Expert Network** and **Operators** offer light-touch support, guidance, and feedback.
- Programs are encouraged to host local meetups or share learnings publicly to inspire others.

Impact measurement includes both **real-world outcomes** (social, ecological, cultural, economic) and **on-chain activity** (funding flows, attestations, hypercerts).

### Stage 5 – Round Closure & Reporting

By **March 2026**, all Local Programs are expected to have **initiated their local funding rounds** and to submit a **progress update or interim round report**.

A synthesis of these reports will form the **Localism Fund Round 01 Report**, shared across the ecosystem as an open public good.

Programs do **not need to have completed all disbursements** by this time, but they should demonstrate that:

- Their local program has **formally launched and begun distributing funds.**
- They can share **early evidence of activities, partnerships, outcomes** and **impact stories** achieved so far.
- They are tracking **both real-world impact and on-chain activity** (e.g. attestations, funding flows, onboarding metrics).

Ultimately the Localism Fund Operator team will compile these updates and reflections into a **Localism Fund Round 01 Outcomes Report** to be a living public record of what worked, what’s emerging, and how Ethereum Localism is taking root globally — helping future programs build from collective experience.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Do I need to already use Ethereum or Web3 tools?

Not necessarily — what matters most is **intention and readiness**.

You should have a clear plan to **learn, adopt, or integrate** Ethereum-based infrastructure into your program (e.g. wallets, attestations, stablecoins, impact tracking, funding and governance tools)

Support and playbooks will be provided to help new teams onboard smoothly.

### What kind of co-funding counts toward the $5k minimum?

You can combine **any legitimate local or external sources**, such as:

- Contributions from community members, NGOs, or local businesses
- Municipal or institutional partnerships
- Other grants or sponsorships
- Crowdfunding or QF campaign proceeds
- Matching commitments from aligned ecosystems

The key is **credibility and traceability** — your co-funding should reflect genuine community or partner buy-in.

### How will matching amounts be calculated?

Each Local Program can receive **up to $20,000 in matching funds**, depending on both the **evaluation score** and the **amount of co-funding secured**.

The **Evaluation Rubric score** determines your **matching multiplier**, which is then applied to the verified amount of co-funding your program has raised.

Programs that demonstrate stronger performance across scope, impact, and capacity — and that have secured larger, credible co-funding commitments — receive proportionally higher matching.

| **Score Range** | **Matching Multiplier** | **Outcome**                              |
| --------------- | ----------------------- | ---------------------------------------- |
| 13–16           | Up to 2×                | Strong candidate, priority funding       |
| 10–12           | ~1.5×                   | Qualified candidate, standard allocation |
| 7–9             | 1× or conditional       | Pending improvements                     |
| < 7             | —                       | Not eligible this cycle                  |

Any unallocated matching will roll into future Localism Fund rounds.

### Can some of the grant funding be used for program operations or development?

Yes. While most of each **Local Grant Program’s** funds are intended for **matching local projects**, a portion can be used to cover **operational and coordination costs.**

Gitcoin’s proposed [**Fair Fees** framework](https://gov.gitcoin.co/t/temp-check-fair-fees-for-gg24/22560) was introduced to make running funding rounds more **sustainable and equitable** for operators and software providers. It recognizes that quality funding programs and rounds requires real time, tools, and infrastructure.

Under this model, a **reasonable percentage (typically 10–20%)** of a round’s budget may be allocated toward essential program operations — such as software costs, evaluation, outreach, reporting, and facilitation. This ensures local programs remain effective and accountable, while avoiding burnout or underfunding for those coordinating the work.

Fair Fees aims to recognize that **smaller rounds naturally require a larger percentage of their matching pool** to cover fixed operational costs, while **larger rounds benefit from economies of scale** and can sustain lower relative % fees. The goal is to ensure fair compensation that reflects real operational effort without being extractive — supporting smaller or experimental rounds while keeping larger ones efficient and impact-focused.

Details of your proposed fee should be detailed in your application to this round.

### What happens after my program finishes?

All funded local programs will publish a **final report** documenting:

- Funds distributed
- Local impact stories
- On-chain metrics (attestations, funding flows, activity)
- Lessons learned for future cohorts

A synthesis of these reports will form the **Localism Fund Round 01 Outcomes Report**, shared across the ecosystem as an open public good.
