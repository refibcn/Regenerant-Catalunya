## Article Outline

### Opening (2-3 paragraphs)

**Hook - The Vision**

> In November 2025, we launched Regenerant Catalunya with an ambitious vision: Could cooperative networks in Catalunya use Web3 tools to govern resources collectively, creating infrastructure for ongoing bioregional finance?
>
> Three months later, having completed Phase 1 with 11 regenerative projects across two networks, we have our first answers. Some confirm our hypotheses. Others surprised us. All are valuable for the emerging field of bioregional finance infrastructure (BioFi).
>
> Here's what we learned.

---

### Section 1: What We Planned vs. What Happened

**The Original Phase 1 Design:**

**We planned:**
- Equal baseline allocation (~€1,000 per project)
- On-chain impact documentation via Karma GAP
- Web3 wallet setup for all projects
- Workshops and office hours for support
- Lightweight evaluation for learning
- Strong engagement from all 11 projects

**What actually happened:**

**The successes:**
- ✅ Phase 1 closing workshop delivered (Jan 19)
- ✅ Projects that engaged deeply got tremendous value
- ✅ Karma GAP proved viable for regenerative impact reporting
- ✅ Web3 wallets set up (with support variations)
- ✅ Rich learnings about cooperative-Web3 integration
- ✅ Foundation built for Phase 2 network governance

**The challenges:**
- ⚠️ Engagement varied significantly across projects
- ⚠️ Technical complexity higher barrier than anticipated for some
- ⚠️ Timeline pressures (holidays, delayed funding) affected participation
- ⚠️ Different network cultures required different approaches
- ⚠️ Impact documentation practice more valuable than compliance reporting

**The surprises:**
- 💡 Projects most engaged were those who saw beyond just funding
- 💡 In-person support absolutely critical for less technical networks
- 💡 Karma GAP as "project resume" resonated more than "funder compliance"
- 💡 Network differences (Miceli vs. La Fundició) more pronounced than expected
- 💡 Cooperative values and Web3 can align - but requires intentional design

### Section 4: Tools and Processes - What Worked, What Didn't

#### Karma GAP for Impact Reporting

**What worked:**
- ✅ Provided public, on-chain record of regenerative activities
- ✅ "Project resume" framing more motivating than compliance
- ✅ Template structure helped projects think systematically about impact
- ✅ Visible to global funders and partners
- ✅ Shareable links for project promotion

**What was difficult:**
- ⚠️ Learning curve steeper than expected
- ⚠️ Some ambiguity about what counts as "activity" vs. "output" vs. "metric"
- ⚠️ Time investment significant for comprehensive documentation
- ⚠️ Platform occasionally buggy or confusing
- ⚠️ Not all projects saw value (felt like bureaucratic requirement)

**Lessons for future:**
- More hands-on support for first submission
- Clearer examples and templates specific to regenerative work
- Emphasize value proposition (portfolio building, not compliance)
- Office hours critical for troubleshooting
- Consider: Karma GAP "office hours" with screen sharing

---

#### Web3 Wallets (Valora, MetaMask, etc.)

**What worked:**
- ✅ Valora's social recovery feature well-suited for communities
- ✅ Most projects successfully set up wallets with support
- ✅ Once set up, receiving funds straightforward
- ✅ Visual guide with screenshots highly valuable
- ✅ Phone-based wallets (Valora) more accessible than desktop

**What was difficult:**
- ⚠️ Initial setup anxiety ("What if I do it wrong?")
- ⚠️ Recovery phrase management responsibility felt heavy
- ⚠️ Some projects wanted ReFi Barcelona to manage wallets for them
- ⚠️ Confusion about different wallet options
- ⚠️ Technical troubleshooting time-intensive

**Lessons for future:**
- Offer in-person setup workshops (not just guides)
- Provide multiple wallet options with clear comparison
- Consider: ReFi Barcelona wallet management service (with clear governance)
- Recovery phrase security workshop needed
- Video tutorials complement written guides

---

#### Workshops & Training

**What worked:**
- ✅ In-person kickoff workshop (Nov 19) created community
- ✅ Network-specific documentation workshops allowed customization
- ✅ Hands-on, step-by-step approach effective
- ✅ Mixing education with practical tasks
- ✅ Bilingual materials (Spanish/Catalan) appreciated

**What was difficult:**
- ⚠️ Scheduling across 11 projects always challenging
- ⚠️ Online workshops less engaging than in-person
- ⚠️ Some projects couldn't attend synchronously
- ⚠️ Balancing depth with time constraints
- ⚠️ Technical difficulties (WiFi, audio, screen sharing)

**Lessons for future:**
- Hybrid format essential (in-person option + online)
- Record all workshops for asynchronous viewing
- Shorter, more frequent sessions better than long workshops
- Build in buffer time for technical issues
- Consider: Workshop series vs. one-offs

---

#### Communication & Support

**What worked:**
- ✅ WhatsApp group for quick questions
- ✅ Email for detailed communications
- ✅ Weekly ops sync for coordination
- ✅ Direct, responsive support from ReFi Barcelona team
- ✅ Multilingual communication

**What was difficult:**
- ⚠️ Information overload at times
- ⚠️ Not all projects checked communications regularly
- ⚠️ Balancing proactive outreach with respecting autonomy
- ⚠️ Different communication preferences across projects
- ⚠️ Clarity on what's required vs. optional

**Lessons for future:**
- Clear communication hierarchy (critical vs. optional)
- Multiple channels but consistent messages
- Visual timelines and checklists
- Regular check-ins with less responsive projects
- Clearer expectations from start

### Section 5: Key Learnings for Bioregional Finance

#### Learning #1: Sense-Making Before Tools

**What we learned:**
> Introducing tools before understanding governance needs leads to resistance or misalignment. Networks needed time to articulate their governance challenges before seeing how Web3 tools might help.

**For Phase 2, we changed:**
- Workshop #4 starts with sense-making (not tool introduction)
- Tools introduced only after networks identify needs
- Networks choose tools based on their context
- Alternative engagement models for networks that don't want certain tools

**Replicable principle:**
- **Always understand governance context before proposing tools**
- Trust networks to know their needs
- Tools should serve communities, not vice versa
- Expect and design for different outcomes per network

---

#### Learning #2: Network Culture Shapes Tool Adoption

**What we learned:**
> Two networks in the same program, same city, similar cooperative values - but dramatically different comfort levels with Web3 tools. Culture matters more than technical capacity.

**Miceli Social:** Tech-forward culture meant fast adoption, interest in advanced features, experimental mindset

**La Fundició:** Territorial-presential culture meant need for simplicity, in-person support, skepticism requiring patience

**Implication:**
> BioFi programs must design for network-specific adaptation from the start. Not just translated materials, but fundamentally different facilitation approaches, support structures, and tool recommendations.

**Replicable principle:**
- **Assess network culture before program design**
- Plan for different approaches per network
- Budget more support time for less technical networks
- Celebrate different paths, not just one "right way"

---

#### Learning #3: In-Person Support Is Non-Negotiable

**What we learned:**
> For networks less comfortable with Web3, written guides and video tutorials are helpful but insufficient. In-person, hands-on support makes the difference between adoption and abandonment.

**What made difference for La Fundició:**
- Being physically present at La Fundició for setup
- Walking through wallet creation computer-by-computer
- Troubleshooting in real-time, in person
- Building trust through physical presence
- Patience and repetition without judgment

**Resource implications:**
> This requires significant facilitation time and budget. But without it, Web3 adoption in traditional cooperative contexts likely fails.

**Replicable principle:**
- **Budget for extensive in-person support** for less technical networks
- Remote-only programs work for tech-native communities, not all communities
- Physical presence builds trust necessary for new tool adoption
- Time investment upfront saves abandonment later

---

#### Learning #4: Purpose Matters More Than Incentives

**What we learned:**
> Projects engaged deeply not primarily for the €1,000 allocation (though appreciated), but because they understood the program as building infrastructure for ongoing bioregional finance.

**Projects that saw program as:**
- **Just funding:** Minimum engagement, compliance mindset, transactional
- **Capacity building:** Deep engagement, learning mindset, collaborative

**We didn't emphasize purpose enough early:**
- Initial communications focused on funding amount
- Should have led with: "Building replicable infrastructure"
- BioFi context introduced too late (Workshop #5 should have been Workshop #1 content)

**Replicable principle:**
- **Lead with purpose and vision, not just incentives**
- Funding attracts; purpose retains and motivates
- Make "pilot program" explicit from day one
- Connect local action to global movement early

---

#### Learning #5: Impact Documentation as Portfolio, Not Compliance

**What we learned:**
> Framing Karma GAP as "building your project's portfolio" rather than "compliance requirement for funding" changed engagement dramatically.

**When framed as compliance:**
- Minimum effort
- Resentment or resistance
- Box-checking mentality
- Value unclear

**When framed as portfolio:**
- Pride in documentation
- Sharing with others
- Ongoing updating beyond requirements
- Clear value (visibility, credibility, future funding)

**One project quote:**
> "[After completing Karma GAP] I realized we've never had a clear, public record of our work. This is actually really valuable for showing what we do."

**Replicable principle:**
- **Frame impact reporting as serving projects, not funders**
- Emphasize benefits: Visibility, credibility, portfolio, future opportunities
- Provide examples of how documentation opens doors
- Celebrate and amplify projects' Karma GAP profiles

---

#### Learning #6: Cooperative Values and Web3 Can Align - Intentionally

**What we learned:**
> There's natural skepticism from cooperative movements toward crypto/Web3 (often seen as extractive, speculative, antithetical to solidarity values). But with intentional design, alignment is possible.

**What enabled alignment:**
- Partnership with trusted local networks (Miceli Social, La Fundició)
- Emphasis on transparency and collective governance (core cooperative values)
- Tools that hide complexity and serve real needs
- ReFi Barcelona as bridge (part of cooperative ecosystem, fluent in Web3)
- Honest about what Web3 can and can't do
- Never forcing adoption

**What would have broken alignment:**
- Imposing tools without consultation
- Using extractive crypto narratives
- Prioritizing technology over community
- Dismissing cooperative wisdom
- Rushing without trust-building

**Replicable principle:**
- **Web3 + cooperatives requires bridge builders**
- Speak both languages fluently
- Foreground shared values (transparency, collective governance, solidarity)
- Be patient with skepticism (often justified given crypto history)
- Prove value through action, not promises

### Section 6: Transition to Phase 2 - What Changes

**From Individual to Collective:**

**Phase 1 was about:**
- Individual projects receiving support
- Learning tools independently
- Building capacity one-by-one
- Equal baseline allocation

**Phase 2 is about:**
- Networks governing resources collectively
- Practicing governance together
- Network-level capacity building
- Shared treasury management (€6k/€5k per network)

**Why this progression matters:**

> Phase 1 couldn't start with collective governance - projects needed to understand tools individually first. But Phase 1 alone wouldn't create sustainable infrastructure - networks needed to practice governing collectively.

> The two-phase structure scaffolds learning: Individual comfort → Collective governance → Sustainable infrastructure.

**What we're testing in Phase 2:**
- Can Miceli Social network govern €6k collectively using Safe multisig?
- Can La Fundició network use Hum Community for participatory budgeting?
- Does the Institutional Development Kit help networks understand their governance before tool adoption?
- What support do networks need for collective governance to work?

**Stakes are higher:**
- Larger amounts (€5-6k vs. €1k)
- Collective decision-making (not individual)
- Real governance practice (not just tool learning)
- Infrastructure that could continue beyond program

### Section 8: For Other Bioregions and Networks

**If you're considering a similar program:**

**Start here:**

**1. Partner with trusted local networks**
- Don't parachute in with Web3 solutions
- Build from existing trust and relationships
- Co-design, don't impose

**2. Design for network-specific adaptation**
- Different networks need different approaches
- Budget for multiple facilitation styles
- One framework, multiple implementations

**3. Sense-making before tools**
- Understand governance context first
- Let needs drive tool selection
- Be open to "no Web3 tools" outcome

**4. Invest in support**
- In-person support for less technical communities
- Office hours and responsive communication
- Patient troubleshooting
- Celebrate small wins

**5. Lead with purpose, not incentives**
- Frame as infrastructure building
- Connect to broader movement (BioFi)
- Emphasize learning and replication value
- Funding attracts; purpose motivates

**6. Document everything, share openly**
- Successes and failures both valuable
- Open-source all materials
- Honest reporting builds ecosystem knowledge
- Your learnings help everyone

**What you'll need:**

**Resources:**
- €20-30k minimum for meaningful pilot (11 projects)
- 6+ months for two-phase structure
- Facilitation team with cooperative + Web3 fluency
- Local partner networks with trust and capacity

**Capacity:**
- 20-30 hours/week facilitation and support
- Workshop design and delivery skills
- Technical troubleshooting capacity
- Documentation and reporting
- Multilingual support (if applicable)

**Patience:**
- Tool adoption takes longer than you think
- Cooperative decision-making has its own pace
- Trust building can't be rushed
- Expect the unexpected
