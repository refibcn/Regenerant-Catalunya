# Workshop 2 Recap: Building Verifiable Impact Documentation with KarmaGap

_Workshop 2 of Regenerant Catalunya's Phase 1 capacity-building program focused on impact documentation using KarmaGap—an on-chain activity and impact reporting platform._

## What is KarmaGap?

**KarmaGap** (recently rebranded as "Karma") is an on-chain activity and impact reporting platform that enables projects to create a living, public "project resume"—making their regenerative impact visible and verifiable to funders, communities, and the broader regenerative finance ecosystem.

Unlike traditional impact reporting that happens in isolated documents or spreadsheets, KarmaGap creates permanent, verifiable records on-chain that can be accessed by anyone, anytime.

## Why Impact Documentation Matters for Regenerative Finance

### Transparent Impact Reporting

All activities and impact metrics are recorded on-chain, creating verifiable records that funders can trust. This transparency:

- Builds credibility with funders
- Enables real-time impact tracking
- Creates accountability without bureaucracy
- Demonstrates long-term commitment to regenerative work

### Tool Integration

KarmaGap connects with other regenerative finance tools, creating an integrated ecosystem:

**COP (Community Operating Protocol)** — For community governance data and decision-making records

**Eco Certs/Hypercerts** — For impact credentials and verification, enabling projects to "mint" their impact achievements

**Silvi** — For tree planting and forest restoration MRV (Measurement, Reporting, Verification), enabling projects to verify and monetize carbon sequestration

This integration means projects don't have to use multiple disconnected systems—everything connects in a verifiable, on-chain ecosystem.

### Global Visibility

Projects can showcase their impact to funders worldwide, opening new funding opportunities beyond traditional grant cycles. This is especially valuable for:

- International funders looking for verified impact
- Web3-native funding rounds
- Impact investors seeking transparent metrics
- Communities wanting to verify project claims

### Long-term Value

Impact documentation becomes a permanent record that projects can use for years to come. This creates:

- A verifiable history of regenerative work
- Credibility for future funding applications
- Evidence for policy advocacy
- Learning resources for other projects

## What Projects Learned in Workshop 2

### Creating Project Profiles

Projects set up their profiles on KarmaGap, including:

- Project description and mission
- Location and bioregional context
- Team and organizational structure
- Contact information and links

### Documenting Activities

Projects learned to document their regenerative activities with:

- **Dates** — Start and end dates for activities
- **Outputs** — What was produced or accomplished
- **Deliverables** — Specific results or products
- **Metrics** — Quantifiable impact indicators

### Setting Impact Metrics

Projects identified and set metrics for tracking their regenerative impact, such as:

- Ecosystem restoration indicators (biodiversity, water quality, vegetation cover)
- Community engagement metrics (participants, events, partnerships)
- Economic impact (jobs created, local spending, cooperative development)
- Social impact (food sovereignty, housing access, community infrastructure)

### Connecting Activities to Regenerative Outcomes

Projects learned to connect their activities to broader regenerative outcomes, demonstrating:

- How individual activities contribute to bioregional regeneration
- The relationship between different types of regenerative work
- Long-term impact beyond immediate outputs
- Systems-level change and transformation

### Building Verifiable Impact Narratives

Projects practiced creating compelling narratives that:

- Tell the story of their regenerative work
- Demonstrate impact with verifiable data
- Connect local work to global regenerative finance
- Build credibility with funders and communities

## The Approach: Building Capacity, Not Just Teaching Tools

We're not just teaching a tool—we're building capacity for ongoing impact documentation. Projects will use KarmaGap beyond this program to:

**Demonstrate impact to future funders**

- Create verifiable records of regenerative work
- Build credibility in the regenerative finance space
- Access new funding opportunities

**Build credibility in regenerative finance**

- Participate in Web3 funding rounds
- Connect with impact verification tools
- Join global regenerative finance networks

**Access new funding opportunities**

- Apply to Web3-native funding rounds
- Attract impact investors seeking transparency
- Participate in network-level governance (Phase 2)

**Create transparent accountability**

- Build trust with communities
- Demonstrate responsible fund management
- Create learning resources for others

## Challenges and Solutions

### Challenge: English-Only Interface

**Solution:** We provided Spanish-language materials and support, with plans for Catalan translations. Breakout groups allowed for personalized assistance in participants' preferred languages.

### Challenge: Complexity for New Users

**Solution:** Step-by-step guidance, templates, and examples. Projects could see real examples from other regenerative projects, making the process more accessible.

### Challenge: Time Investment

**Solution:** We emphasized that impact documentation is an investment in future funding. Projects that complete comprehensive KarmaGap profiles will have a significant advantage in future funding rounds.

## What's Next

After Workshop 2, projects are working on completing their KarmaGap profiles and documenting their 2025 activities. Workshop 3 will focus on impact metrics and regenerative measurement methodologies, helping projects refine their impact documentation.

**Office Hours:** Throughout December, we're offering office hours for projects needing additional support with KarmaGap setup and documentation.
