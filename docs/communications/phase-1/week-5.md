## Newsletter Article

# Phase 1 Progress Update: Building Infrastructure for Ongoing Bioregional Regeneration

_As we approach the end of Phase 1, we're reflecting on what we've accomplished and what projects are learning._

## What We've Accomplished

### Three Capacity-Building Workshops

**Workshop 1: Web3 Wallet Setup**

- Projects learned to set up wallets with social recovery (Valora, Minipay)
- Understood transparent fund management and global access
- Connected wallets to impact reporting tools

**Workshop 2: KarmaGap Impact Documentation**

- Projects created profiles and documented activities
- Learned to set impact metrics and track progress
- Built verifiable impact narratives

**Workshop 3: Regenerative Measurement Methodologies**

- Projects refined their impact metrics
- Learned to connect activities to regenerative outcomes
- Built capacity for ongoing impact documentation

### Eleven Projects Engaged

All 11 projects have:

- Set up Web3 wallets for secure fund management
- Created KarmaGap profiles for impact documentation
- Begun documenting their regenerative activities
- Connected with other projects in the network

### Impact Documentation

Projects are creating verifiable, on-chain records of their regenerative activities using KarmaGap. This includes:

- Project profiles and mission statements
- Documented activities with dates, outputs, and deliverables
- Impact metrics and tracking progress
- Connections to regenerative outcomes

### Network Building

Projects are connecting with each other and building relationships across Catalonia:

- Sharing knowledge and experiences
- Identifying collaboration opportunities
- Building collective capacity
- Preparing for Phase 2 network-level governance

## What Projects Are Learning

### Impact Measurement

Projects are learning to:

- Set appropriate metrics for their regenerative work
- Track progress toward regenerative outcomes
- Document both quantitative and qualitative impact
- Connect local work to bioregional regeneration

**Examples:**

- Ecosystem restoration: biodiversity, water quality, vegetation cover
- Community infrastructure: participants, events, partnerships
- Food sovereignty: meals served, community engagement, producer relationships
- Housing cooperatives: units created, community participation, affordability

### Tool Integration

Projects are learning to:

- Connect wallets to KarmaGap for impact reporting
- Integrate with other regenerative finance tools
- Build verifiable records of their work
- Create transparent accountability

**Tool Ecosystem:**

- Wallets (Valora, Minipay) for fund management
- KarmaGap for impact documentation
- Future integration with COP, Eco Certs, Silvi

### Global Visibility

Projects are building profiles that:

- Showcase their regenerative impact to funders worldwide
- Create verifiable records of their work
- Enable participation in Web3 funding rounds
- Build credibility in regenerative finance

### Long-term Value

Projects are creating:

- Permanent records of their regenerative work
- Credibility for future funding applications
- Evidence for policy advocacy
- Learning resources for other projects

## The Impact: Building Infrastructure, Not Just Providing Funding

This program isn't just about providing funding—it's about building infrastructure for ongoing regenerative finance. Projects are learning tools and methodologies they'll use for years to come:

**Beyond This Program:**

- Projects can use KarmaGap for future funding applications
- Wallets enable participation in Web3 funding rounds
- Impact documentation builds credibility with funders
- Network connections enable ongoing collaboration

**Replicable Model:**

- Other bioregions can adapt this approach
- Open-source knowledge products enable replication
- Methodology can be applied to other funding rounds
- Creates sustainable infrastructure for regeneration

## Challenges and Learnings

### Challenge: English-Only Tools

**Learning:** We provided Spanish-language materials and support, with personalized assistance in breakout groups. This highlighted the need for multilingual tool support in regenerative finance.

### Challenge: Complexity for New Users

**Learning:** Step-by-step guidance, templates, and examples made the process more accessible. Projects appreciated the hands-on support and personalized assistance.

### Challenge: Time Investment

**Learning:** Projects understood that impact documentation is an investment in future funding. Those who completed comprehensive profiles will have advantages in future funding rounds.

### Challenge: Balancing Support with Autonomy

**Learning:** We found the right balance between providing support and allowing projects to work independently. Office hours and breakout groups provided flexibility.

## What's Next: Phase 2 Preview

**Phase 2 (Jan-Feb 2026): Network-Level Collective Governance**

In Phase 2, networks will collectively govern additional funds using Web3 governance tools:

**Miceli Social Network (~€6k)**

- Rural regenerative projects
- Collective decision-making on fund allocation
- Web3 governance tools (Gardens, Safe multisig)

**La Fundició/Keras Buti Network (~€5k)**

- Urban regenerative projects
- Collective decision-making on fund allocation
- Web3 governance tools for transparent governance

**What This Creates:**

- Model for ongoing bioregional regeneration
- Infrastructure for future funding rounds
- Experience with Web3 collective governance
- Replicable approach for other bioregions

## Reflection

Phase 1 has been about building capacity and infrastructure. Projects aren't just receiving funding—they're learning tools and methodologies that will serve them for years to come.

This creates a replicable model for bioregional regeneration that other regions can adapt. The combination of traditional cooperative values with cutting-edge Web3 tools positions Regenerant Catalunya as a bridge between worlds.
