## Newsletter Article

# Project Spotlight: La Olla Community Kitchen - Urban Regenerative Work in L'Hospitalet

_This is the second in our series spotlighting the 11 regenerative projects supported by Regenerant Catalunya._

## The Project

**La Olla Community Kitchen** operates from Casal Comunitari La Llenadita in L'Hospitalet de Llobregat—Catalonia's second most populous municipality and one of Europe's most densely populated cities. This project demonstrates how urban regenerative work addresses food sovereignty and community infrastructure in challenging urban environments.

**Their regenerative work includes:**

### Food Sovereignty

- Providing 100+ ecological meals at accessible prices
- Making healthy, organic food available to communities that need it most
- Using locally-sourced, organic ingredients
- Creating alternatives to industrial food systems

### Community Infrastructure

- Operating from a community center (Casal Comunitari La Llenadita)
- Creating a space for community gathering and mutual support
- Building relationships within the neighborhood
- Facilitating food knowledge sharing and education

### Ecological Practices

- Using organic, locally-sourced ingredients
- Sustainable cooking methods and waste reduction
- Supporting local producers and farmers
- Demonstrating alternatives to industrial food systems

### Community Engagement

- Building relationships within the neighborhood
- Food knowledge sharing and education
- Creating spaces for community gathering
- Facilitating mutual support networks

## The Challenge

Urban regenerative projects like La Olla face unique challenges:

**High Costs**

- Urban real estate is expensive
- Organic ingredients cost more than industrial alternatives
- Balancing accessibility with sustainability requires careful pricing

**Limited Space**

- Dense urban environments offer limited space for community activities
- Need to maximize impact in small physical spaces
- Competing with commercial uses for space

**Accessibility vs. Sustainability**

- Need to keep prices accessible to communities that need it most
- Organic and local ingredients are more expensive
- Finding the balance between accessibility and sustainability

**Impact Documentation**

- Difficult to measure community impact quantitatively
- Food sovereignty outcomes are long-term and qualitative
- Limited resources for comprehensive impact measurement

## How Regenerant Catalunya Supports Their Work

### Phase 1 Funding

Projects receive **€1,000-1,500** in Phase 1 to support ongoing operations. For La Olla, this funding helps:

- Continue providing accessible ecological meals
- Invest in community infrastructure
- Build capacity for future funding

### Impact Documentation

Projects are learning to document their community impact and food sovereignty outcomes using **KarmaGap**. This helps La Olla:

- Demonstrate community impact to future funders
- Build credibility in the regenerative finance space
- Access new funding opportunities
- Create transparent accountability

### Network Connection

Through the program, La Olla connects with other food sovereignty projects across Catalonia, creating opportunities for:

- Knowledge sharing and collaboration
- Collective advocacy for food sovereignty
- Network-level governance (Phase 2)
- Ongoing support and community

### Capacity Building

By learning Web3 tools and impact documentation, La Olla is building skills for:

- Transparent impact reporting
- Future funding applications
- Network-level participation
- Ongoing regenerative finance

## The Impact

With Regenerant Catalunya's support, La Olla can:

- Continue providing accessible ecological meals
- Build a verifiable record of their community impact
- Connect with a network of food sovereignty projects
- Access new funding opportunities
- Create sustainable funding infrastructure

## Urban vs. Rural Regenerative Work

La Olla represents urban regenerative work, which differs from rural projects like Fluvià River Restoration:

**Urban Challenges:**

- Higher costs and limited space
- Dense populations and diverse needs
- Competing with commercial systems
- Need for immediate, visible impact

**Urban Opportunities:**

- Direct access to communities
- Potential for rapid scaling
- Integration with existing infrastructure
- Visibility and demonstration value

**Common Ground:**

- Both build community resilience
- Both address systemic challenges
- Both create alternatives to extractive systems
- Both need sustainable funding infrastructure

## What's Next

In Phase 2, La Olla will participate in network-level collective governance through La Fundició/Keras Buti network, helping decide how additional funds are allocated across urban regenerative projects. This creates a model for ongoing bioregional regeneration that includes both rural and urban work.
